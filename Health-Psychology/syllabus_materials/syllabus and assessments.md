## Module Home

#### Hi Mark! Welcome to the Health Psychology Module.

![decorative](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1651498/course/section/293770/pexels-leticia-alvares-1805702-33359326.jpg)

Health Psychology is an interdisciplinary field, concerned with the application of psychological knowledge to health, illness and healthcare. Its primary purpose is to understand and improve the health and wellbeing of individuals and communities. This module will introduce you to psychological approaches to understanding health and illness, and human interactions with healthcare systems. Throughout the module, psychological knowledge, theory and research related to health and chronic illness will be presented, discussed and critically appraised.

You will have the opportunity to critically engage with key issues in the field of health psychology, including theories of health behaviour, behaviour change and a range of perspectives related to psychology of chronic illness management. This module will draw on research from a range of epistemologies; therefore, you can expect to examine case studies, qualitative studies, cross-sectional surveys, longitudinal surveys, clinical trials and metanalyses. At the end of this module you will have developed critical evaluation skills relevant to theoretical and empirical issues which involve the application of psychology to health and illness.

#### Module Overview

The main focus of this module will be to discuss the role of psychology in promoting health behaviours including diet, physical activity and sleep and how unhealthy behaviours such as smoking can be limited. We will also consider how health psychology can be used to understand chronic illnesses (such as cancer, arthritis, dementia and diabetes), disease management and the professional issues that health psychologists and allied healthcare professionals face when working in health and social care settings.

#### In this module we shall cover:

- An introduction to health psychology and approaches to health psychology, which covers the role of a health psychologist and the different approaches to understanding health, as well as understanding health behaviours and predicting health behaviours (Unit 1).
- The prediction of health behaviours and how social cognition models can be applied to health promotion campaigns (Unit 2).
- How theoretical models can be linked to health campaigns and why theory driven approaches may be beneficial in delivery health messages (Unit 3).
- Health promotion interventions followed by individual differences and health, which covers defining quality of life as a construct and understanding the factors which determine health inequalities and their outcomes (Unit 4).
- Stress, health and coping strategies, including definitions of stress and how we can explain our ability to cope with stress (Unit 5).
- Chronic illnesses, which includes defining and describing chronic illness, as well as self-management and medicine taking behaviours (Units 6 and 7).
- Psychological processes at the end of life, which looks at the process of ageing and the impacts on our health and wellbeing (Unit 8).
- The role of psychology in supporting healthcare professionals, which looks into healthcare professional stress and compassion fatigue, and patient-healthcare relationships (Unit 9).

#### Module Aims and Learning Outcomes

#### The aims of this module are:

- Understanding of how key health psychology theories and models can be applied to individuals and populations with a view to improving health.
- Knowledge of the history of health psychology and critical understanding of how different approaches to health psychology can inform our understanding of health and illness related behaviours.
- Use of research to critically evaluate how key issues such as stress, health inequalities, quality of life and lifestyle choices affect health beliefs, health behaviours and health outcomes.
- Critical appraisal of key health psychology theories for their ability to map the predictors of health behaviour, and understand the application of theory to strategies to improve health and/or limit the impact of illness.
- Critical understanding of the role that psychology plays across a range of stages in chronic illness.
- Understanding of a range of professional issues faced by healthcare professionals and health psychologists, and how psychology can be used to inform, manage and resolve.

#### On the successful completion of this module, you should be able to meet the following module learning outcomes:

1. Critically analyse the current research evidence related to health and/or illness beliefs and/or health behaviours.
2. Understand key issues related to health, health/illness beliefs and health/illness behaviours and critically appraise their impact on individuals, groups and society.
3. Critically evaluate approaches to health promotion, behaviour change and illness management, and understand how theory and research can inform the development of health psychology interventions.
4. Understand the role health psychologists, and related professionals (including nurses and allied healthcare professionals and health educators), and the skills required to develop evidence-based practice and theory driven interventions.

# Unit 3 and 4 Collaborative Learning Question

The purpose of this collaborative learning activity is a type of discussion activity which promotes higher-level thinking, communication skills, self-management and leadership skills. This is achieved through student interaction, engagement with course materials and wider reading around the module topics. This activity allows students to understand a variety of diverse perspectives on topics in psychology and helps to prepare you for real life social and employment opportunities.

After the tutor has opened the discussion with a post, students are required to offer an **initial post** that demonstrates their answer to the question, then read and respond to two of their peers' initial posts, making comments and asking questions, **always using an academic tone and backing up your ideas with APA referencing.**

The initial post should be labelled '**Initial Post**' and the two follow-up responses should be labelled as **'Peer Response 1'** and **'Peer Response 2'**. Along with the initial post, the two responses will be judged according to all grading criteria.

#### Collaborative Learning Question

**Initial post:** Your first task is to design a theory led health promotion poster. You can choose one of the following health promotion issues to focus on:

- Discouraging excessive alcohol consumption.
- Promoting 30 minutes of exercise per-day.
- Reducing daily sugar intake.

As the poster must be theory led, you must use one of the following theories to inform the creation of your poster:

- The health belief model.
- The theory of planned behaviour.
- Self-efficacy theory.

**Follow-up response:** In the second part of the assessment, you must critically respond to two of your peers' posters. Your responses should draw on theories of critical thinking and learning, and focus on the content of their posters and executive summaries, not just the poster design. You should aim to post 200 words per response.

##### Instructions on Creating a Health Promotion Poster:

Your poster must be engaging, easy to read and understand, and informed by psychological research related to your chosen health promotion issues.

To create your poster, you should use an A4 size document on Microsoft Word or use 1 slide of a PowerPoint Presentation file. It can also be helpful to save your poster as a PDF file for submission. Theory led health promotion posters are visually different to traditional academic posters that may be presented at a conference. For this assignment, your poster should be creative, attractive to look at, and easily grab the reader’s attention. This can be achieved by not overloading the poster with text, and including concise health messages, statements etc. It should be clear to the reader’s what the message of your poster is i.e. what behaviour you are trying to change. You do not need a reference list on your poster.

##### Instructions for the Executive Summary:

Your poster will be accompanied by a 500-word executive summary. This summary must explain how you have applied theory to your chosen health promotion topic **and** explain how you have used research to inform the health promotion messages being communicated in your poster. The references included in the academic poster must be referred to in the executive summary.

# End of Module Essay (Unit 9)

This assessment is due at the **end of Unit 9**. The word count is 2,200 words.

#### End of Module Essay Question

For this assignment, you are required to write an essay that will focus on one specific chronic illness (of your choosing) and to draw on psychological theory and current research to evidence your arguments.

Your proposal must address all of the following questions:

1. What is the impact of your selected chronic illness on the individual (including prevalence, adjustment, quality of life)?
2. What are the self-management challenges associated with your selected chronic illness, and can theoretical models tell us about the way individuals cope with chronic illness?
3. Critically evaluate current behaviour change interventions in your selected chronic illness.
4. Based on your understanding of theory, research, and available interventions (covered in questions 1, 2, and 3), propose an intervention to promote optimal wellbeing in chronic illness.

Your proposal must demonstrate your ability to assimilate a range and depth of research knowledge and understanding of your selected illness and its impact. You should use appropriate psychological theories and research to support your proposal and show the development of clear conclusions which are supported by evidence. #### Academic Integrity and Plagiarism

We take academic integrity very seriously. Academic integrity means acting with fairness and honesty, giving credit to others where you are referring to their ideas or research and respecting the work of others. Plagiarism is defined as: 'Using or copying the work of others (whether written, printed or in any other form) without proper acknowledgement'. Before you finalise your assignment take time to check that all your statements are backed up with supporting evidence, that all sources you use - whether referring to their ideas, quoting directly or paraphrasing - are correctly referenced in the text. Correct use of referencing acknowledges the academic whose work has informed yours, enables the reader to find the sources you have used and demonstrates your ability to find and analyse relevant information.

Failure to properly acknowledge the work of others is an academic offence and may result in your work incurring a penalty or, in the most serious cases, you being removed from the course for academic dishonesty.

If you are unsure about referencing or plagiarism there are useful resources available in the Study Skills Hub which is accessible from the menu on the left hand side. If you are still experiencing difficulties with academic integrity then you can contact the Study Skills Team for individualised support [**studyskills-kol@kaplan.com**](mailto:studyskills-kol@kaplan.com).

Joanne is 30 years old, has a high body mass index and is classed as obese. Joanne’s doctor wants her to start exercising to reduce her risk of developing diabetes. The doctor has the following leaflet about diabetes and exercise which she is planning to give to Joanne.
