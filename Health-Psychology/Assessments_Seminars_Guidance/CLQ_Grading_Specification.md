# Health Psychology CLQ Grading Specification

Version 1.0 · 9 October 2026

**Assessment:** Unit 3–4 Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ): theory-led health-promotion poster, 500-word executive summary and two peer responses.

**Status:** Assessment-specific configuration of the repository-level `GRADING_FEEDBACK_ENGINE_SPEC.md`. The official University of Essex Online rubric and assessment brief remain authoritative. The rules below operationalise those documents for consistent cohort marking.

## 2. Official criteria and weightings

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues | 30% |
| Critical Analysis and Evaluation | 20% |
| Communication | 20% |
| Reading and Referencing | 20% |
| Presentation Style and Structure | 10% |

Each criterion has exactly four agreed diagnostic subcriteria. Within a criterion, the four subcriteria are equally weighted for diagnostic aggregation only.

## 3. Agreed diagnostic subcriteria

### C1. Knowledge and Understanding — 30%

- **C1.1** Accurate understanding of the chosen health-behaviour theory
- **C1.2** Understanding of the target health behaviour
- **C1.3** Understanding of theory-based behaviour change
- **C1.4** Integration of course concepts and research

### C2. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 20%

- **C2.1** Critical evaluation of the selected theory
- **C2.2** Critical use of empirical evidence
- **C2.3** Justification of poster and intervention choices
- **C2.4** Critical engagement in peer responses

### C3. Communication — 20%

- **C3.1** Clear behavioural message in the poster
- **C3.2** Executive summary explains the theory–research–message link
- **C3.3** Peer responses are constructive and substantive
- **C3.4** Audience-appropriate communication

### C4. Reading and Referencing — 20%

- **C4.1** Breadth of academic reading
- **C4.2** Quality and relevance of research-informed sources
- **C4.3** Evidence-to-claim alignment
- **C4.4** APA citation and reference accuracy

### C5. Presentation Style and Structure — 10%

- **C5.1** Formal format and completeness
- **C5.2** Legibility and text density
- **C5.3** Visual organisation and engagement
- **C5.4** Structure, writing accuracy and technical presentation

## 4. Competence coding

Score every subcriterion independently before cohort moderation.

| Code | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Not demonstrated, fundamentally incorrect, irrelevant, or absent where required |
| **0.5** | Partly demonstrated but materially incomplete, inaccurate, weakly justified or uneven |
| **1** | Adequately and substantively demonstrated at Level 5; minor weaknesses may remain |

Each code must be accompanied by a concise evidence note and location.

These competence codes are diagnostic and are **not** converted mechanically into percentage marks.

---

## 5. Independent subcriterion quality marks

Assign a separate quality mark `q_icj` to each subcriterion after the 0/0.5/1 competence judgement.

### 5.1 Normal operating range

For this assessment, **subcriterion quality marks should normally fall between 45 and 85**.

This is a tutor calibration policy, not a University grade-band rule.

The purpose is to avoid artificial extremes when a short CLQ task provides only limited evidence for very fine-grained percentage distinctions.

Typical interpretation:

| Quality mark | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| **80–85** | Rare exceptional performance for this task; unusually strong accuracy, synthesis, judgement or communication |
| **70–79** | Strong first-class performance; developed, critical, independent and well justified |
| **60–69** | Good 2:1 performance; competent, relevant and developed with identifiable limitations |
| **50–59** | Adequate 2:2 performance; relevant but limited, uneven or insufficiently developed |
| **45–49** | Weak but meaningful evidence; substantial limitations |
| **40–44** | Borderline / marginal evidence; substantive weaknesses requiring close review |
| **Below 40** | Dramatically weak, absent or seriously flawed performance; mandatory tutor-review flag |

### 5.2 Relationship between competence code and quality mark

- **Code 1:** normally supports a quality mark from about 55–85, depending on depth and sophistication.
- **Code 0.5:** normally supports about 45–59. A mark of 60+ requires a clear written rationale for unusually strong partial evidence.
- **Code 0:** normally supports 0–44 depending on whether there is some relevant but non-demonstrative material. A wholly absent subcriterion should normally receive 0.

Do not force a subcriterion into 45–85 if the evidence is dramatically failing. Very low values are permitted where academically justified, but must be flagged.

### 5.3 Mandatory flags

Flag for tutor review where any of the following occurs:

- any subcriterion quality mark is **below 40**;
- any official criterion quality mark is **below 40**;
- the independent or processed overall is **below 40**;
- there are multiple competence codes of 0 within one official criterion;
- an apparently required submission component is missing;
- the evidence is inaccessible or unresolved;
- any proposed overall mark is 80–85.

---

## 5.4 Module-reading engagement rule

The CLQ is designed to assess engagement with module learning as well as generic knowledge of health promotion.

For marking purposes:

- substantive use of **Barile et al. (2021)**, a relevant model-specific module source, and appropriate wider evidence should be visible in strong submissions;
- **Ilic & Rowe (2013)** is particularly relevant where the student justifies poster communication/design;
- **Borrell-Carrió et al. (2004)** may support contextual or biopsychosocial critique;
- a reading counts only when its concepts, findings, methods or limitations are used in the student's reasoning.

A source listed but not substantively used does not satisfy the reading-engagement expectation.

**Band guidance:**
- **60+**: normally requires meaningful engagement with relevant module/seminar scholarship, not only generic external sources;
- **70+**: normally requires integrated use of a model-specific source, seminar-led evidence and wider literature;
- **80–85**: requires unusually strong synthesis and critical use of module and independent scholarship.

If there is **no meaningful engagement with identifiable module/seminar reading**, normally:
- C4.1 cannot score 1;
- C1.4 should be constrained because integration of course concepts/research is not fully demonstrated;
- Reading and Referencing should not enter the First-class band unless the evidence clearly shows equivalent engagement with the designated module scholarship.

Do not enforce a mechanical citation count.

## 6. Required CLQ components and eligibility

The submission package consists of:

1. Initial post containing the theory-led health-promotion poster;
2. 500-word executive summary;
3. Peer Response 1;
4. Peer Response 2.

The assessment brief states that all three posts — the initial post plus both peer responses — must be submitted to pass.

Therefore:

- **missing both peer responses:** not eligible for automatic pass calibration; hold and flag for tutor review;
- **missing one peer response:** not eligible for the normal borderline uplift; hold and flag for tutor review;
- **missing initial post/poster or executive summary:** hold and flag for tutor review;
- an unreadable or inaccessible component is `unresolved`, not automatically zero, until checked.

Do not use cohort moderation to conceal non-completion of a required assessment component.

---

## 7. Poster-format checks

Formal requirements from the brief:

- A4 document or one PowerPoint slide;
- engaging and easy to read;
- concise health messages;
- clear target behaviour and intended behaviour change;
- explicitly theory-led;
- research-informed;
- no reference list required on the poster itself.

Internal legibility guidance, not an official University minimum:

- title approximately 24–32 pt;
- major headings approximately 16–20 pt;
- substantive body text normally at least 11–12 pt on A4;
- actual legibility takes precedence where exact font sizes cannot be verified.

The poster should normally contain:

- clear title/headline;
- identifiable target behaviour;
- concise health-promotion message(s);
- visible application of the selected theory;
- research-informed claims or recommendations;
- clear call to action or behavioural recommendation;
- coherent visual hierarchy.

Do not require conventional conference-poster sections such as Introduction, Method, Results and Discussion.

## 8. Independent criterion and overall marks

For student `i`, criterion `c`, subcriterion `j`:

```text
X_ic = mean(x_icj)                         # diagnostic competence proportion
Q_ic = mean(q_icj)                         # independent criterion quality mark
B_i  = sum_c(w_c * Q_ic)                   # independent weighted quality overall
```

Official criterion weights:

```text
w_C1 = 0.30
w_C2 = 0.20
w_C3 = 0.20
w_C4 = 0.20
w_C5 = 0.10
```

`B_i` is the academic-quality basis for cohort moderation. `X_ic` and the 0/0.5/1 matrix remain the competence audit.

## 9. Cohort calibration targets

### 9.1 Target mean

The target academic cohort mean is:

```text
M = 65
```

The target applies to the complete assessed cohort **after academic eligibility checks and before any administrative word-count penalty**.

### 9.2 Desired spread

The desired ordinary cohort range is approximately:

```text
45–85
```

This is a **soft descriptive target**, not a quota or hard clipping rule.

For numerical moderation, use:

```text
target_population_sd = 8
```

as the initial soft spread target, because an SD around 8 is broadly compatible with a cohort centred at 65 and most ordinary outcomes lying in approximately the mid-40s to low-80s.

Do not manufacture a minimum of 45. Dramatically failing work may sit below 45 and must be flagged. Do not manufacture an 85 either; 80–85 requires exceptional evidence.

### 9.3 Distribution policy

- Do not force a Gaussian distribution.
- Do not create failures or exceptional grades to improve the histogram.
- Preserve meaningful ties.
- Preserve rank ordering where evidence and eligibility are comparable.
- Use one common constrained affine transformation across criteria, following the generic engine.
- Criterion movement from the independent quality mark is normally limited to ±5 points.

---

## 10. Borderline-fail policy

The undergraduate pass threshold is 40.

The tutor preference for this CLQ is that **genuine “just fail” numerical outcomes should normally be reported as 40 rather than 38 or 39**, provided the work is substantively eligible to pass.

Operational rule:

```text
if processed_overall in [38, 39]
and all required submission components are present
and no essential criterion is wholly absent
and no unresolved evidence remains:
    academic_overall = 40
    record borderline_uplift = true
else:
    retain evidence-based result or hold for review
```

This is a tutor moderation convention, not an institutional grade-band rule.

A student must **not** be uplifted to 40 merely to achieve the target mean.

### 10.1 Extreme / substantive failures

Any processed overall below 38 is treated as an **extreme or substantive fail candidate** and is not finalised automatically.

Required action:

1. verify all files and components were read correctly;
2. re-check all 20 subcriteria and evidence locations;
3. verify no extraction or formatting issue suppressed assessable evidence;
4. produce a concise reason for the fail;
5. **flag for tutor review before release**.

The tutor decides the final mark for these cases.

If a required assessment component is missing, the case is also held for tutor review regardless of the numerical total.

---

## 11. Exceptional grades

- Routine upper bound: **79**
- Exceptional range: **80–85**
- Absolute maximum: **85**

Any criterion or overall mark of 80–85 requires a written evidence-based rationale demonstrating unusually strong performance for a Level 5 CLQ.

There is no quota for 80+ grades.

---

## 12. Moderation procedure

1. Score all 20 subcriteria using 0/0.5/1 without reference to the target distribution.
2. Assign independent quality marks to all 20 subcriteria, normally within 45–85 unless the evidence clearly warrants a lower mark.
3. Aggregate to five independent criterion marks and the independent weighted overall.
4. Complete a cross-cohort calibration pass by comparing evidence on the same subcriterion across students.
5. Review:
   - all subcriteria below 40;
   - all criterion marks below 40;
   - all overall fail candidates;
   - all 80+ candidates;
   - missing required components;
   - unusually inconsistent competence/quality combinations.
6. Freeze the evidence matrix and independent marks.
7. Apply constrained common-affine cohort moderation toward mean 65 and population SD 8, subject to evidence bounds, eligibility and maxima.
8. Apply the 38–39 → 40 borderline rule only after academic moderation and only to eligible submissions.
9. Hold all remaining overall marks below 40 for tutor review.
10. Jointly round criterion marks so the displayed weighted criterion marks reproduce the final academic overall as closely as mathematically feasible.
11. Report the achieved mean, SD, minimum, maximum and any activated bounds or tutor-review flags.

---

## 13. Word-count penalty

The CLQ brief states that exceeding the applicable word-count limit/range by **more than 10%** results in a **10 percentage-point reduction**.

Apply only after the academic grade has been moderated and any borderline rule resolved.

```text
penalty = 10 if verified_assessed_word_count > 1.10 * authorised_upper_limit else 0
final_grade = max(0, academic_grade - penalty)
```

The current brief specifies:

- executive summary: 500 words;
- peer responses: approximately 200 words each.

Before applying any penalty, verify the authorised counting method and whether the limit is applied separately or collectively. If this is unclear, flag the case rather than inventing a counting rule.

Do not renormalise the cohort after administrative penalties.

## 14. Feedback format

For each student, provide:

- exactly three concise strengths;
- exactly three priority areas for improvement;
- one paragraph for each of the five official criteria;
- official criterion percentages only in student-facing feedback;
- overall academic grade and final grade if a penalty changes it;
- two-sentence personalised closing observation.


### Feedback voice and seminar-linking

Student-facing feedback should sound like feedback from the tutor who has taught the module, not a detached rubric summary.

Where it is genuinely relevant to the student's work, make **specific links back to the taught seminars**, for example:

- “As we covered in Seminar 1, …”
- “This connects well with the point we discussed in Seminar 2 about …”
- “For the next assignment, return to the approach we practised in Seminar 3 …”

Rules:

- use the **actual seminar number and topic** supported by the module's seminar materials / `Seminar_Led_Reading_Integration.md`;
- make the seminar reference analytically useful — it should remind the student of a concept, method, reading or activity that helps explain the feedback;
- do not force a seminar reference into every criterion paragraph;
- normally include **at least one useful seminar connection in the overall feedback when an appropriate connection exists**;
- never claim something was taught in a seminar if the source materials do not support that claim.

Use brief, natural encouragement where warranted. Appropriate phrases include:

- “Great job here.”
- “Excellent work.”
- “Nicely done.”
- “This is a real strength.”
- “Good work on this.”
- “You handled this well.”
- “This is developing well.”

Praise must be **evidence-calibrated**:

- reserve “Excellent work” and similarly strong praise for genuinely excellent / First-class evidence;
- use warmer but more measured phrases such as “Nicely done” or “Good work on this” for solid achievement;
- avoid generic praise that is not tied to something the student actually did;
- do not make every paragraph begin or end with praise.

The overall tone should be **warm, encouraging, specific and academically direct**. Areas for improvement should be framed constructively and, where possible, tell the student what to do next rather than merely naming a weakness.

### Required wording conventions in student feedback

These are hard style rules for all student-facing feedback:

- **Never use “scholarly”.** Use **“academic”**, **“high-quality academic”**, **“research literature”**, or **“high-quality sources”** as appropriate.
- Use natural contractions in feedback: **“don't”, “won't”, “can't”, “isn't”, “doesn't”, “you've”, “you're”** rather than unnecessarily formal **“do not”, “will not”, “cannot”, “is not”, “does not”, “you have”, “you are”**. Keep the tone professional, but conversational.
- **Never use “designated textbook”.** Refer to it as the **“module textbook”**.
- These wording rules apply to headings, criterion paragraphs, strengths, improvement points and the closing observation.


Do not show the student:

- 0/0.5/1 diagnostic codes;
- subcriterion quality marks;
- cohort normalisation calculations;
- tutor-review flags intended only for internal moderation.

Use British English and address the student directly.

- 20 subcriterion quality marks per student;
- evidence location and concise rationale for every subcriterion;
- five independent criterion marks;
- independent weighted overall;

- borderline-uplift flag;
- missing-component / unresolved flags;
- extreme-fail tutor-review flag;

The cohort report must show:

- population SD;
- minimum and maximum;
- band counts;

- subcriterion quality distributions;

## 17. Release gates

No grade is released until:

- all required assessment components are accounted for;
- all 20 subcriteria are scored or explicitly unresolved;
- every sub-40 subcriterion has been reviewed;
- every overall fail candidate has been checked;
- every extreme-fail candidate has tutor approval;
- every 80–85 mark has an exceptional-quality rationale;
- displayed criterion marks reproduce the released overall;
- any word-count penalty is independently verified;
- cohort mean, SD, minimum and maximum are reported honestly;
- feedback matches the same final grade-sheet run.
