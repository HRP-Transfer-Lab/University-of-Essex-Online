# CLQ Academic Poster: Criteria and Diagnostic Subcriteria

## Assessment

**Unit 3–4 Collaborative Learning Question**

Students must:

- design a theory-led health promotion poster;
- submit a **500-word executive summary** explaining how theory and research informed the poster;
- submit **two peer responses**, approximately 200 words each;
- use one of the specified theories:
  - Health Belief Model;
  - Theory of Planned Behaviour;
  - Self-efficacy theory;
- focus on one of the specified health-promotion behaviours:
  - discouraging excessive alcohol consumption;
  - promoting 30 minutes of exercise per day;
  - reducing daily sugar intake.

Source documents include:

- `HPY CLQ Academic Poster Grading Criteria.pdf`
- `syllabus and assessments.md`
- module reading list and seminar/formative materials

## Official criteria and weightings

1. **Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues — 30%**
2. **Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 20%**
3. **Communication — 20%**
4. **Reading and Referencing — 20%**
5. **Presentation Style and Structure — 10%**

The four diagnostic subcriteria below sit underneath each official criterion.

---

## 1. Knowledge and Understanding — 30%

### 1.1 Accurate understanding of the chosen health-behaviour theory
The student demonstrates accurate knowledge of the major constructs, assumptions and behavioural mechanisms of the selected Health Belief Model, Theory of Planned Behaviour or self-efficacy approach.

### 1.2 Understanding of the target health behaviour
The student demonstrates relevant knowledge of the chosen behaviour, including important psychological, social or contextual determinants and its relationship to health.

### 1.3 Understanding of theory-based behaviour change
The student explains how the selected theoretical constructs are expected to influence behaviour and why they are relevant to the chosen health-promotion problem.

### 1.4 Integration of course concepts and research
The student integrates appropriate health-psychology concepts and research to provide a coherent rationale for the poster rather than presenting disconnected definitions or claims.

---

## 2. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 20%

### 2.1 Critical evaluation of the selected theory
The student considers the strengths, limitations, assumptions or boundary conditions of the selected theoretical model rather than describing it uncritically.

### 2.2 Critical use of empirical evidence
The student evaluates the quality, relevance or consistency of research used to justify the poster's messages and behavioural recommendations.

### 2.3 Justification of poster and intervention choices
The student explains why specific messages, prompts, framings or calls to action were chosen and links those design decisions to theory and evidence.

### 2.4 Critical engagement in peer responses
The two required peer responses go beyond praise or description by identifying strengths, weaknesses, theoretical issues, evidential limitations or constructive alternatives in peers' posters and executive summaries.

---

## 3. Communication — 20%

### 3.1 Clear behavioural message in the poster
The poster communicates the target behaviour and intended behaviour change clearly, concisely and appropriately for the intended audience.

### 3.2 Executive summary explains the theory–research–message link
The executive summary clearly explains how theoretical constructs and research evidence informed the health-promotion messages and design choices.

### 3.3 Peer responses are constructive and substantive
The peer responses are relevant, respectful, academically framed and add intellectual value through questions, critique, suggestions or comparison with theory/evidence.

### 3.4 Audience-appropriate communication
Across the poster, executive summary and peer responses, the style, terminology and level of complexity are appropriate to the audience and purpose.

---

## 4. Reading and Referencing — 20%

### 4.1 Breadth of academic reading
The work uses an appropriate range of academic sources, including module material and meaningful wider reading beyond essential texts where appropriate.

### 4.2 Quality and relevance of research-informed sources
The student relies primarily on suitable psychological and health-related academic literature, with good use of peer-reviewed journal research and appropriate authoritative sources.

### 4.3 Evidence-to-claim alignment
Empirical and theoretical sources genuinely support the claims, health messages and intervention rationale for which they are cited.

### 4.4 APA citation and reference accuracy
In-text citations and references in the executive summary and peer responses are accurate, consistent and appropriately presented in APA style.

---

## 5. Presentation Style and Structure — 10%

### 5.1 Formal format and completeness
The submission includes all required components: a single-page/slide health-promotion poster, a 500-word executive summary, and two peer responses. The poster follows the required A4-equivalent format specified in the assessment brief.

### 5.2 Legibility and text density
The poster is readily legible at normal viewing size, uses sufficiently large and consistent type, and avoids excessive text density.

**Recommended internal legibility benchmark, not an official University minimum unless separately specified:**

- main title: approximately **24–32 pt**;
- major headings: approximately **16–20 pt**;
- substantive body text: normally **at least 11–12 pt** on A4;
- smaller text should only be used where still clearly legible.

Actual legibility takes precedence over numerical font size where exact size cannot be verified.

### 5.3 Visual organisation and engagement
The poster uses layout, hierarchy, spacing, headings, concise wording and appropriate visual elements to attract attention and support rapid comprehension of the intended health message.

### 5.4 Structure, writing accuracy and technical presentation
The poster, executive summary and peer responses are coherently organised and professionally presented, with accurate grammar, spelling and punctuation.

---

# Poster content and formal requirements

## Requirements supported by the assessment brief

The poster should:

- be presented as an **A4 document** or **one PowerPoint slide**;
- be engaging, easy to read and easy to understand;
- avoid excessive text;
- use concise health messages or statements;
- make the target behaviour and intended behaviour change clear;
- be explicitly theory-led;
- be informed by psychological research;
- **not require a reference list on the poster itself**.

The executive summary must:

- be **500 words**;
- explain how theory was applied;
- explain how research informed the health-promotion messages;
- refer to the sources underpinning the poster.

The collaborative component must include:

- one initial post;
- two peer responses;
- appropriate academic tone;
- APA referencing.

## Expected poster components for consistent marking

These are diagnostic expectations derived from the brief rather than mandatory section headings:

- clear title/headline;
- clearly identifiable target behaviour;
- concise health-promotion message(s);
- visible application of the selected theory;
- research-informed claims or recommendations;
- identifiable call to action or behavioural recommendation;
- appropriate visual support where useful;
- coherent visual hierarchy.

Do **not** require conventional academic-poster sections such as Introduction, Method, Results and Discussion. This assignment is a theory-led health-promotion poster rather than a conference research poster.

---

# Diagnostic use

For each subcriterion:

- **1** = fully and convincingly satisfied
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously satisfied
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

Use these scores diagnostically. Award the official criterion percentage holistically using the University's grade-band descriptors.
