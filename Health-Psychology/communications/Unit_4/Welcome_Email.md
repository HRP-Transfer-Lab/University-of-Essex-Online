**Subject:** Welcome to Unit 4 — individual differences, health and your CLQ peer responses

Hello everyone,

Welcome to **Unit 4: Individual Differences and Health**.

This week, we build on our work on health promotion by considering why people can experience health and illness differently. A useful question to keep in mind is: **why might the same illness, or the same health-promotion message, have very different implications for different people?**

## This week's focus

We'll explore **health-related quality of life**: how people assess their wellbeing in relation to their health, how this is measured, and how researchers use changes in quality of life to evaluate outcomes. As you read, consider what these measures capture that more traditional indicators of health might miss.

We'll also examine **health inequalities**, including the role of socioeconomic circumstances, and consider the psychological impact of unequal health opportunities and outcomes. The aim is not simply to identify differences between people, but to think critically about the circumstances and barriers that shape those differences.

Alongside this, we'll consider **co-morbidity**, particularly the combination of mental and physical health problems, and explore health psychology **across the lifespan**, from childhood and adolescence through to older adulthood.

## Your CLQ: this is the peer-response week

The **Units 3–4 Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ)** is now in its **second and final week**. Your initial poster and **500-word executive summary** should have been posted during Unit 3. This week's assessed task is to **critically respond to two peers' posters and executive summaries**, with **200 words per response**.

Please label your contributions **“Peer Response 1”** and **“Peer Response 2”**, and post both in the [CLQ discussion forum](https://www.my-course.co.uk/mod/url/view.php?id=1427177) **by the end of Unit 4**. Check the assessment area for the exact submission date and time.

Your responses should go beyond general praise or comments about colours and layout. Focus on the content of both the poster and its executive summary:

- **Theory:** Is the chosen model genuinely applied? Which specific construct is the message targeting, and is the proposed link to behaviour clear?
- **Evidence:** Does the research support the health message and the claims being made? Are there limitations or alternative explanations worth considering?
- **Audience and context:** How well does the message fit its intended audience? Could resources, life stage, health circumstances or other barriers affect its relevance or feasibility?

Draw on **relevant theories of critical thinking and learning**, as required by the brief, and support your discussion with academic sources using **APA referencing**. A useful approach is to identify a specific strength, examine a limitation using evidence, and offer a constructive suggestion or question. Aim to develop the discussion rather than simply agree or disagree.

Remember that the CLQ is worth **30% of the module mark**, and **all three posts are required to pass this assessment**. Responses posted after the deadline will not be assessed. Keep within the word-count requirements: exceeding the limit or range by more than 10% incurs a deduction of 10 grade points.
