Welcome to **Health Psychology**. I’m Mark, and I’ll be your tutor for the module.

Over the next nine units, we’ll look at how psychological theory and research can help us understand health, illness and healthcare — from health behaviours and behaviour change, through stress and chronic illness, to patient–professional relationships and the wellbeing of healthcare staff.

## Unit 1: Introduction to Health Psychology

This first unit gives us the foundations for the rest of the module. In particular, we’ll consider:

- what we mean by *health*;
- the biomedical and biopsychosocial approaches to health and illness;
- the role of the health psychologist;
- how psychological, biological and social factors can interact;
- an introduction to theories used to understand and predict health behaviour.

A key theme throughout the module will be moving beyond simply **describing** theories and research. I’ll be encouraging you to ask questions such as: *What does this model explain well? What does it miss? What evidence supports it? How could it be applied in practice?* These are the same kinds of skills you’ll later use in the assessments.

## Seminar 1 preparation

For the first seminar, please focus particularly on the biopsychosocial model.

Our core seminar reading is:

**Borrell-Carrió, F., Suchman, A. L., & Epstein, R. M. (2004). _The biopsychosocial model 25 years later: Principles, practice, and scientific inquiry._ The Annals of Family Medicine, 2(6), 576–582.**

Full text: https://www.annfammed.org/content/2/6/576

You do not need to memorise the paper. As you read it, just come prepared to think about one question:

> **What does the biopsychosocial model add that a purely biomedical explanation does not?**

We’ll use the paper in the seminar to think about how biological, psychological and social influences interact, rather than treating them as three separate lists.

If you want to go a little further, the supplementary reading is:

**Lehman, B. J., David, D. M., & Gruber, J. A. (2017). _Rethinking the biopsychosocial model of health: Understanding health as a dynamic system._ Social and Personality Psychology Compass, 11(8), e12328.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12328

## Unit 1 formative activity

There is also a **Formative Discussion Forum** in Unit 1.

Using the WHO definition of health, you’ll consider in what ways different individuals might be described as healthy or unhealthy across physical, mental and social dimensions.

As you work through it, think particularly about:

- Are physical, mental and social aspects of health really easy to separate?
- What other factors might influence an individual’s health?
- Does the biopsychosocial model help us make better sense of these cases?

The formative activity is useful preparation for the kind of theory-to-application thinking we’ll use throughout the module.

## Looking ahead to the first assessment

Your first summative assessment begins in **Unit 3** and runs across Units 3–4.

You’ll create a **theory-led health-promotion poster**, supported by a **500-word executive summary**, and then critically respond to two peers’ posters.

You’ll choose one of three health behaviours:

- excessive alcohol consumption;
- physical activity;
- daily sugar intake;

and apply one of three theoretical approaches:

- the Health Belief Model;
- the Theory of Planned Behaviour;
- self-efficacy theory.

We’ll build towards this gradually. In Units 1 and 2, the priority is to understand how health-psychology models work, what they can explain, and where their limitations lie. The strongest assessment work will not simply name a theory: it will show how the theory, evidence and health message fit together.

We’ll also make regular use of the module readings in seminars, so that by the time you reach the assessment you will already have practised using research to support, apply and critique theoretical ideas.

Please do work through the Unit 1 materials before the seminar if you can, and bring along any questions.
