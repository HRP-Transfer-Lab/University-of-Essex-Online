## Unit 2: Predicting Health Behaviour

This week, we'll explore how psychological theories can help us understand and predict health-related decisions, from exercising regularly and eating healthily to avoiding behaviours that increase our risk of illness.

We'll focus particularly on three important theoretical approaches:

- **The Health Belief Model (HBM):** How our perceptions of health risks, the benefits of changing behaviour and the barriers we face influence our decisions.
- **The Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB):** How attitudes, social expectations and perceived behavioural control contribute to our intentions and actions.
- **Self-efficacy theory:** Why our confidence in our ability to change behaviour can be so important in determining whether we actually do so.

As you work through the material, I'd encourage you to think critically about these approaches. How well do they explain real-world behaviour? Can knowing someone's attitudes or intentions reliably predict what they will do? And what might these models overlook about the social and environmental circumstances in which people make health decisions?

These questions will become particularly important when we start applying theory to health-promotion interventions.

## This week's reading

The essential reading includes:

**Straub (2023), *Health Psychology: A Biopsychosocial Approach*, Part 3, Chapters 6 and 7**, covering primary prevention, positive psychology, exercise, sleep and injury prevention.

**Morrison and Bennett (2016), *An Introduction to Health Psychology*, Chapter 5**, which provides further coverage of psychological approaches to understanding health behaviour.

There are also some useful additional readings on the Health Belief Model, planned behaviour and self-efficacy, including work by Bandura, Janz and Becker, and Montaño and Kasprzyk.

As you read, try to identify the key psychological constructs within each model and consider how they might be used to encourage healthier behaviour.

## Unit 2 formative activity: Joanne and the Health Belief Model

There's a useful case study in this week's materials involving **Joanne, a 30-year-old woman whose doctor wants to encourage her to exercise more regularly to reduce her risk of developing diabetes**.

You'll be asked to consider how Joanne's existing beliefs might affect her willingness to exercise, and how the doctor's advice and health-promotion leaflet could be improved using the Health Belief Model.

[Access the Unit 2 Health Belief Model activity](https://www.my-course.co.uk/mod/page/view.php?id=1427164)

I'd particularly encourage you to think about this question:

**How could we use psychological theory to create a more persuasive and effective health message, rather than simply telling someone what they should do?**

Think about Joanne's perceived susceptibility to diabetes, the benefits and barriers she associates with exercise, and the practical changes that might help her feel more capable of becoming active.

This is a good opportunity to practise applying theory to a real-world situation.

## Looking ahead: Your first assessment

Next week, in **Unit 3**, you'll begin your first summative assessment, the Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ), which continues through Unit 4.

You'll design a theory-led health-promotion poster addressing one of three behaviours: excessive alcohol consumption, physical activity or daily sugar intake.

Your poster will need to apply one of the three theoretical approaches we're studying this week, supported by a **500-word executive summary** explaining how theory and research have informed your health message. You'll also critically respond to two classmates' posters.

Your initial post is due during Unit 3, with your two peer responses completed during Unit 4.

So, as you work through Unit 2, it would be useful to start thinking about which health behaviour interests you and which model might offer the most helpful framework for designing an intervention.

We'll also develop these ideas further in **Seminar 2 on health promotion**, where we'll consider research on theory-based health messages and how we can evaluate their effectiveness.

Remember, the aim isn't simply to describe a psychological theory, but to show **how its principles can inform practical behaviour change, and how research evidence supports or challenges that application**.
