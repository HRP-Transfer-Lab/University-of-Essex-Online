# Psychobiology & Neuroscience Poster Conference — Grading Instructions

Version 1.1 · 23 October 2026  
Level 7 MSc | University of Essex Online | Units 11–12 Poster Conference

**Status:** authoritative grading specification for the Poster Conference component following the Research Poster assessment.

This specification is based on:
- University of Essex Online, *Grading Criteria: Poster Conference* (Version 1, April 2021);
- current module instructions for Units 10–12;
- the historical Piper Instructions for poster comments and responses;
- the reusable cohort-calibration principles in the repository-wide grading engine.

It preserves the four official Poster Conference criteria and their equal 25% weightings. It operationalises those criteria separately for:
1. three critical Comments on peers’ posters; and
2. three Responses to comments on the student’s own poster.

The two components are marked independently and then combined 50:50.

---

## 1. Official criterion structure

The official Poster Conference rubric contains four equally weighted criteria:

1. **Application of Knowledge and Understanding — 25%**
2. **Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 25%**
3. **Ethical Considerations — 25%**
4. **Animation in Discussion Forums — 25%**

Do not introduce additional weighted criteria.

Evidence use, APA referencing, clarity and scholarly writing are evaluated within these four criteria rather than being added as a fifth criterion.

---

## 2. Overall assessment architecture

Calculate three grades.

### Component A — Comments grade

```text
Comments Overall =
0.25 × Comments Knowledge
+ 0.25 × Comments Methodology
+ 0.25 × Comments Ethics
+ 0.25 × Comments Animation
```

### Component B — Responses grade

```text
Responses Overall =
0.25 × Responses Knowledge
+ 0.25 × Responses Methodology
+ 0.25 × Responses Ethics
+ 0.25 × Responses Animation
```

### Combined Poster Conference grade

```text
Poster Conference Overall =
(Comments Overall + Responses Overall) / 2
```

Use the unrounded component overalls for the 50:50 combination, then round the final overall to a whole percentage using ordinary nearest-integer rounding.

Released criterion percentages should be whole numbers and should reproduce the released component overall as closely as mathematically possible. Where joint rounding is required, preserve the academic evidence and documented ordering rather than applying an unexplained hidden uplift.

# 3. Component A — Critical Comments on peers’ posters

The student submits three approximately 200-word critical comments on three different peer posters.

For this cohort, treat **Comment 1, Comment 2 and Comment 3 as three explicit scoring slots**. Assess each submitted Comment against the four official criteria using the relevant internal subcriteria below. A missing required Comment receives **0 for all four criterion scores for that slot**.

After scoring all three slots, calculate each Comments criterion grade as the arithmetic mean of that criterion across Comment 1, Comment 2 and Comment 3. The Comments Overall is then the 25/25/25/25 weighted mean of the four resulting criterion grades.

This slot-based approach replaces the earlier collective-only treatment because it makes missing required contributions explicit and auditable.

## A1. Application of Knowledge and Understanding — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### A1.1 Scientific understanding
Does the student accurately understand and apply the relevant psychobiological or neuropsychological concepts and mechanisms in the posters they critique?

Reward:
- accurate use of biological, neural, physiological or neuropsychological concepts;
- correct linkage between biological and psychological/behavioural levels;
- discipline-appropriate interpretation.

Do not reward technical terminology that is fluent but scientifically inaccurate.

### A1.2 Evaluation of rationale and argument
Does the student evaluate whether the proposed study follows logically from the literature and whether the rationale is scientifically sound?

Look for:
- evaluation rather than summary;
- identification of unsupported inferential steps;
- recognition of a strong or weak mechanistic link;
- consideration of alternative explanations or boundary conditions where appropriate.

### A1.3 Expected findings and take-home message
Can the student identify what the study is actually expected to show and evaluate whether the proposed take-home message is justified?

Look for:
- distinction between expected result and demonstrated result;
- appropriate restraint about causality;
- judgement about whether conclusions follow from design and evidence.

### A1.4 Evidence-based critique
Does the student support important criticisms, qualifications or recommendations with relevant psychological literature?

A citation alone does not establish quality. Reward literature that is used to reason, compare, challenge, qualify or justify.

---

## A2. Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### A2.1 Research question and hypothesis identification
Does the student correctly identify the poster’s principal research question and hypothesis/prediction, even where the poster itself is imperfectly phrased?

### A2.2 Design and operationalisation
Does the student evaluate whether the:
- design;
- sample/recruitment;
- predictor/independent variable;
- outcome/dependent variable;
- biological/neuropsychological measure;
- behavioural/psychological measure

actually address the research question?

### A2.3 Analysis and inference
Does the student evaluate whether the proposed analysis could test the stated hypothesis and whether the permitted inference matches the design?

Apply the module rule that:
- correlational, cross-sectional and regression designs support association/prediction;
- they do not establish experimental causality merely because covariates are controlled.

### A2.4 Methodological development
Does the student propose a specific and workable improvement, such as:
- a better measure;
- a control;
- an analytic term;
- a sampling change;
- a design modification;
- a clearer operational definition;
- a targeted follow-up study?

Generic suggestions such as “use a larger sample” receive limited credit unless the student explains why the change matters.

---

## A3. Ethical Considerations — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### A3.1 Identification of study-specific ethics
Does the student recognise the actual ethical issues created by the proposed population, topic, procedure, intervention or measurement?

Do not require generic discussion of every possible ethical issue.

### A3.2 BPS-based ethical reasoning
Does the student reason consistently with the BPS principles of:
- respect for autonomy, privacy and dignity;
- scientific integrity;
- social responsibility;
- maximising benefit and minimising harm?

Naming “BPS ethics” without applying the principles is limited evidence.

### A3.3 Participant protection and data governance
Where relevant, does the student appropriately evaluate:
- informed consent;
- withdrawal;
- vulnerability/capacity;
- coercion or power imbalance;
- distress;
- confidentiality/anonymity;
- data handling;
- debriefing;
- referral/safeguarding;
- neuro-specific risks?

### A3.4 Proportionate ethical improvement
Does the student identify realistic safeguards or recognise where the poster already manages risks appropriately?

Judge ethics proportionately. Across the three comments, students should not be forced to manufacture an ethics criticism where no meaningful ethics issue is present.

---

## A4. Animation in Discussion Forums — 25%

Interpret “animation” as the quality of scholarly conference engagement, not simply post count.

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### A4.1 Specific engagement
Does the student engage directly with substantive features of the individual poster rather than producing generic praise or criticism?

### A4.2 Constructive scholarly dialogue
Does the student combine challenge with useful development and frame criticism in a professional manner likely to advance discussion?

### A4.3 Actionability
Does the student provide:
- a meaningful question;
- a specific alternative;
- a defensible recommendation;
- or another contribution that gives the poster author something substantive to respond to?

### A4.4 Breadth across the three comments
Are all three required comments individually tailored, substantive and collectively useful to the conference?

Repeated templates, generic praise or near-identical criticisms across all posters constrain this criterion.

---

# 4. Component B — Responses to comments on own poster

The student submits three approximately 200-word responses to comments received on their own poster.

For this cohort, treat **Response 1, Response 2 and Response 3 as three explicit scoring slots**. Assess each submitted Response against the four official criteria using the relevant internal subcriteria below. A missing required Response receives **0 for all four criterion scores for that slot**.

After scoring all three slots, calculate each Responses criterion grade as the arithmetic mean of that criterion across Response 1, Response 2 and Response 3. The Responses Overall is then the 25/25/25/25 weighted mean of the four resulting criterion grades.

The central question is:

**Can the student use critique of their own proposal to reason scientifically, defend what is defensible, recognise what is not, and improve the proposal?**

A strong response does not have to agree with the peer. Reasoned disagreement may demonstrate excellent critical engagement.

---

## B1. Application of Knowledge and Understanding — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### B1.1 Understanding of the critique
Does the student correctly identify the substantive scientific issue raised by the commenter rather than responding only to surface wording?

### B1.2 Knowledge-based response
Does the student use relevant psychobiological/neuropsychological knowledge to:
- accept;
- qualify;
- clarify;
- or challenge

the criticism?

### B1.3 Evidence-based defence or revision
Does the student use relevant literature when defending the original rationale or explaining why it should be revised?

Reward evidence used as part of reasoning, not merely added as citation decoration.

### B1.4 Reflective conceptual development
Does the response show that the critique has refined the student’s understanding of:
- the rationale;
- mechanism;
- research question;
- hypothesis;
- expected findings;
- or permissible conclusions?

---

## B2. Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### B2.1 Methodological diagnosis
Does the student correctly understand the methodological issue raised by the peer?

### B2.2 Consequences for validity and inference
Does the student explain why the issue matters for:
- construct validity;
- internal/external validity;
- confounding;
- measurement;
- statistical inference;
- feasibility;
- or generalisability?

### B2.3 Concrete methodological response
Where warranted, does the student propose a specific revision to:
- sampling;
- design;
- operationalisation;
- controls;
- procedure;
- analysis;
- or interpretation?

### B2.4 Reasoned judgement about revision
Can the student distinguish:
- a necessary correction;
- a useful but optional improvement;
- a future-study extension;
- or a criticism that does not justify changing the present proposal?

Reward justified retention of the original method where appropriate.

---

## B3. Ethical Considerations — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### B3.1 Recognition of ethical implications
Does the student recognise ethical consequences of the peer’s critique or of any proposed methodological change?

### B3.2 Application to own proposal
Does the student apply BPS ethical reasoning specifically to their own:
- design;
- population;
- measures;
- intervention;
- recruitment;
- or data?

### B3.3 Concrete safeguard
Where an ethical weakness is identified, does the student propose a realistic safeguard or management strategy?

### B3.4 Proportionate reflection
Does the student show reasoned judgement about risk, burden, consent, privacy, dignity or scientific integrity without inventing irrelevant problems?

**Fairness rule:** if none of the three peer comments raises ethics and no ethical issue is materially implicated by the discussion, do not penalise the student for failing to manufacture an ethics response. Judge the criterion from relevant evidence available across the three responses.

---

## B4. Animation in Conference Contributions — 25%

Assess four internal subcriteria:

### B4.1 Direct responsiveness
Does the student directly address the named commenter and the substantive point(s) raised rather than simply thanking them?

### B4.2 Scholarly dialogue
Does the student engage respectfully but critically, explaining agreement, disagreement or qualification?

### B4.3 Learning and change
Does the student state:
- what they would change in the proposal/poster;
- what they would clarify;
- what they would investigate further;
- or why they would retain the original approach?

### B4.4 Contribution across all three responses
Do all three responses meaningfully advance the discussion and demonstrate sustained engagement rather than perfunctory replies?

# 5. Internal diagnostic scoring

For every one of the 32 subcriteria:

## Competence code

```text
0   = absent, seriously incorrect, or not assessable
0.5 = partially, unevenly, or ambiguously demonstrated
1   = adequately demonstrated
```

The competence code is an audit of whether the required skill is demonstrated. It is **not** an arithmetic grade formula.

## Independent quality mark

Assign a separate evidence-based academic-quality mark to every subcriterion, normally in five-point steps.

The quality mark reflects:
- depth;
- accuracy;
- critical reasoning;
- synthesis;
- methodological judgement;
- evidential support;
- independence;
- and appropriateness at Level 7.

Do not mechanically map:
- 0 → 0%;
- 0.5 → 50%;
- 1 → 100%.

A code of 1 permits a wide range of academic quality.

---

# 6. Post-level and criterion calculation

Each of the three required Comment slots and each of the three required Response slots is scored separately.

For a submitted post, derive its four criterion scores from the relevant internal subcriteria. Within a criterion, the internal quality marks are equally weighted unless a subcriterion is genuinely not applicable; in that case document the reason and average the applicable evidence rather than inventing a zero.

For a missing required post:

```text
Knowledge = 0
Methodology = 0
Ethics = 0
Animation = 0
```

For each component, average the same criterion across the three required posts:

```text
Comments Knowledge =
(Comment1_K + Comment2_K + Comment3_K) / 3

Responses Knowledge =
(Response1_K + Response2_K + Response3_K) / 3
```

and likewise for Methodology, Ethics and Animation.

Then calculate:

```text
Component Overall =
0.25 × Knowledge
+ 0.25 × Methodology
+ 0.25 × Ethics
+ 0.25 × Animation
```

Because all four criteria and all three post slots are equally weighted, this is mathematically equivalent to averaging the three post-level overall grades, but the criterion-first calculation is retained for transparent feedback.

Keep the competence matrix, post-level quality matrix, component criterion grades and component overalls separate for audit purposes.

# 7. Missing-post and completion rule

The module instructions state that students are expected to submit all **3 critical Comments** and all **3 Responses**.

For this cohort, the agreed operational treatment is:

1. Maintain six explicit graded slots: Comment 1–3 and Response 1–3.
2. A missing required slot is scored **0 across all four official criteria**.
3. Do not impute performance from the student's other posts into the missing slot.
4. Do not replace a missing graded post with an informal/unmarked contribution unless the tutor explicitly designates it as a graded substitute.
5. Record the missing contribution transparently in the audit and student feedback where relevant.
6. The slot-zero treatment is the numerical consequence used in the grade calculation for this cohort. Do not apply an additional automatic 49 cap on top of the same missing post unless the tutor later instructs that the University's mandatory-completion rule must also be enforced administratively at release.

# 8. Word count and administrative penalty

The module instructions describe each Comment and Response as **approximately 200 words** and also state that a word-count penalty applies where the formal assessment limit/range is exceeded by more than 10%.

Do not infer a formal 200-word maximum solely from the word “approximately”.

Operational rule:

1. Record the word count of each required graded Comment and Response.
2. Use substantial over- or under-development as evidence relevant to clarity, focus and quality where appropriate.
3. Apply a formal 10-grade-point administrative penalty only when the authorised assessment limit/range applicable to these posts is confirmed from the assessment specification/handbook.
4. Apply any administrative penalty after academic grading, 50:50 combination and pass-eligibility checks.
5. Report the pre-penalty and final penalised grade separately.

There is no penalty merely for falling below the approximate word count, although very short posts may naturally lack sufficient evidence for high criterion marks.

---

# 9. Referencing and evidence

The assessment instructions require the posts to be evidence based and supported with psychological literature, with APA referencing.

Do not create a fifth Referencing criterion.

Evaluate evidence use within the criterion it supports:

- unsupported scientific claims primarily constrain **Application of Knowledge and Understanding**;
- unsupported methodological claims constrain **Research Methodology**;
- unsupported or inaccurate ethics claims constrain **Ethical Considerations**;
- generic, poorly substantiated posting may also constrain **Animation**.

APA mechanics should be judged proportionately. Minor citation-format errors should not be penalised repeatedly across criteria.

Do not infer misconduct from writing style or AI detection. Flag only observable citation, attribution or source-integrity concerns.

---

# 10. Grade range and calibration policy

Operational Level 7 orientation:

- **80–85:** rare outstanding work showing exceptional insight, evidence use, methodological judgement and scholarly dialogue;
- **70–79:** excellent, critically informed and independent Master’s-level work;
- **60–69:** good to very good, developed and generally well-justified work;
- **50–59:** satisfactory Master’s-level achievement with important limitations or uneven development;
- **below 50:** retain only where the component genuinely fails to demonstrate the minimum postgraduate standard, or where the mandatory completion rule determines the final assessment outcome.

**85 is the absolute ceiling.**

Use **79 as the routine upper ceiling**. Any component criterion or component overall in the 80–85 range requires an explicit exceptional-quality rationale confirmed during cross-cohort calibration.

Academic fails should be exceptional. Ordinary weakness, brevity, one poor post or one weak criterion should normally produce a low-pass component where the minimum Level 7 standard is nevertheless demonstrated.

Do not manufacture passes where the work genuinely fails to meet the postgraduate standard.

---

# 11. Cross-cohort calibration

Calibrate Comments and Responses **separately before combining them**.

For each component:

1. Complete independent evidence scoring blind to the desired distribution.
2. Compare students on the same subcriteria.
3. Review:
   - proposed fails;
   - all marks of 80+;
   - unusual outliers;
   - exact/near ties;
   - disagreement between competence and quality;
   - systematic examiner severity;
   - cases where peer-comment quality may have constrained the opportunity to respond.
4. Apply revised interpretations consistently to all affected students.
5. Freeze the calibrated evidence matrices before numerical moderation.

Do not use rank-to-normal conversion, fixed band quotas, random tie-breaking or student identity to create spread.

---

# 12. Normed cohort moderation

Set the reference mean for **Comments** and **Responses** independently before numerical moderation.

A likely starting target for this conversion Master’s cohort is approximately **67–68**, but the exact target must be confirmed after reviewing the raw distributions.

Unless another spread is specified, population SD 7 may be used as a soft moderation target.

Use the same constrained affine moderation principles as the current grading engine:

- common positive transformation;
- evidence-supported criterion bounds;
- no rewriting of competence codes;
- no forced normality;
- no movement beyond what the evidence can support;
- 85 absolute ceiling;
- preserve meaningful ties and ordering;
- avoid academic fails where work demonstrates the minimum Level 7 standard.

If the requested mean/spread is incompatible with the evidence, preserve academic validity and report the nearest defensible distribution.

---

# 13. Special fairness rules for the Responses component

The Responses component depends partly on the quality and content of comments received from peers.

Therefore:

1. Do not penalise a student because a peer failed to raise a sophisticated issue.
2. Judge how well the student uses the opportunities actually present in the comments.
3. Where a peer comment is superficial, reward the student if they deepen the issue independently with evidence and reflection.
4. Do not require an ethics discussion where no ethics issue is raised or reasonably implicated.
5. Do not reward agreement for its own sake; justified disagreement may be stronger than uncritical acceptance.
6. A student's proposed revision should be evaluated for scientific merit, not for whether it complies with the peer’s preference.

# 14. Student-facing feedback format

Use a single feedback report containing both components.

Recommended order:

1. Student name/ID.
2. **Strengths** — exactly three concise bullets across the complete Poster Conference contribution.
3. **Areas for Improvement** — exactly three prioritised, actionable bullets.
4. **Comments: Application of Knowledge and Understanding — XX%**
5. One connected paragraph.
6. **Comments: Research Methodology — XX%**
7. One connected paragraph.
8. **Comments: Ethical Considerations — XX%**
9. One connected paragraph.
10. **Comments: Animation in Discussion Forums — XX%**
11. One connected paragraph.
12. **Comments Overall — XX%**
13. **Responses: Application of Knowledge and Understanding — XX%**
14. One connected paragraph.
15. **Responses: Research Methodology — XX%**
16. One connected paragraph.
17. **Responses: Ethical Considerations — XX%**
18. One connected paragraph.
19. **Responses: Animation in Discussion Forums — XX%**
20. One connected paragraph.
21. **Responses Overall — XX%**
22. **Poster Conference Overall — XX%**
23. Short overall judgement including a specific personal observation about the student’s strongest contribution, change in thinking, or handling of peer critique.

Use British English and address the student directly as “you”.

Do not expose the internal subcriterion codes or quality marks in student-facing feedback.

# 15. Marking workflow

Use this sequence:

```text
ingest graded conference posts
→ identify 3 Comments + 3 Responses
→ verify deadlines and completion
→ record word counts and references
→ evidence-score 16 Comment subcriteria
→ evidence-score 16 Response subcriteria
→ calculate provisional four-criterion Comments grade
→ calculate provisional four-criterion Responses grade
→ cross-cohort calibrate Comments
→ cross-cohort calibrate Responses
→ freeze evidence judgements
→ moderate Comments distribution
→ moderate Responses distribution
→ reconcile whole-number criterion grades
→ calculate Comments Overall
→ calculate Responses Overall
→ average the two unrounded component grades 50:50
→ apply completion rule
→ apply any authorised administrative penalty
→ generate feedback
→ tutor moderation/release
```

---

# 16. Release checks

Before release confirm:

- exactly 3 graded Comments are present or completion flag applied;
- exactly 3 graded Responses are present or completion flag applied;
- all 32 internal subcriteria have evidence-based judgements or are marked not applicable with justification;
- four official criterion grades are present for Comments;
- four official criterion grades are present for Responses;
- each component overall uses 25/25/25/25 weighting;
- final Poster Conference overall uses the unrounded 50:50 component average;
- 85 ceiling has been enforced;
- any 80–85 mark has an exceptional-quality rationale;
- sub-50 academic marks have been manually reviewed;
- peer-comment quality has not unfairly constrained Response grades;
- ethics has been judged proportionately;
- evidence/referencing has been judged within the four official criteria rather than double-penalised;
- word-count penalty has only been applied where the formal authorised limit/range is confirmed;
- any 3+3 completion cap is shown separately;
- displayed grades and overall calculations have been independently rechecked;
- final feedback uses British English and directly addresses the student.
