# Psychobiology & Neuroscience Research Poster — Grading Instructions

Version 2.0 · 23 October 2026  
Level 7 MSc | University of Essex Online | Research-proposal poster

**Status:** authoritative grading specification for the October 2026 poster cohort.

This specification retains the six official weighted criteria and poster-specific rules in `PNS/grading_specs.md`, but supersedes that earlier file for this cohort where the two differ. It also incorporates the cohort-calibration and normed-grading method in the repository-wide `GRADING_FEEDBACK_ENGINE_SPEC.md`.

## 1. Official criterion structure

- Knowledge and Understanding — 20%
- Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 20%
- Ethical Considerations — 20%
- Presentation and Communication Skills — 20%
- Criticality — 10%
- Academic Integrity — 10%

Each criterion retains the four operational subcriteria defined in `PNS/grading_specs.md`. The official University rubric and current assignment brief remain the primary authorities.

## 2. Poster-genre calibration

Treat the submission as a concise A3 research-proposal poster, not a full research report compressed onto one page.

Require enough information to evaluate the scientific chain:

**rationale → research question/aim → hypothesis → operational variables → design/procedure → analysis → expected pattern → interpretation**

Do not penalise merely because every procedural detail, statistical assumption, possible confound, analytic caveat or follow-up study is not written out. A missing detail should materially reduce a mark when it prevents the rationale, design, analysis, ethics, expected result or interpretation from being evaluated, or where the omitted point is central to validity.

Concise wording, diagrams and compact figures may communicate material that would require more prose in a report.

## 3. Technical poster checks

Inspect the original PDF/PPTX and a full-page render.

- Exactly one A3 sheet: approximately 420 × 297 mm landscape or 297 × 420 mm portrait, allowing trivial export tolerance.
- Main communicative text should normally be at least 20 pt.
- References, DOIs and compact image/source credits may normally be about 9–14 pt where legible.
- A3/font/readability issues are assessed within Presentation and Communication, not through an invented separate overall penalty.
- If exact font size cannot be established, record the technical limitation and judge observable legibility rather than assuming non-compliance.

## 4. Methodological feasibility rules

For these online Master's conversion students:

- fMRI, PET and structural MRI are not acceptable as proposed primary data-collection methods.
- Consumer EEG may be acceptable where access, signal quality, preprocessing and the intended component/feature are credibly justified.
- Appropriate neuropsychological tests may provide the biological/neuropsychological side of the study where theoretically justified.
- Secondary-data proposals are judged on data access, data quality, construct validity and analytic feasibility.
- Correlational, cross-sectional and regression designs support association/prediction, not experimental causal inference.
- Covariates do not by themselves remove confounding or convert association into causation.

## 5. Internal evidence scoring

For each of the 24 subcriteria record:

1. **Competence code:** `0 / 0.5 / 1`
   - 0 = absent, seriously incorrect or not assessable
   - 0.5 = partial, uneven or ambiguous
   - 1 = adequately demonstrated
2. **Independent quality mark**, normally in five-point steps, reflecting depth, precision, synthesis, criticality, independence and methodological justification.
3. Concise supporting evidence from the poster.
4. Any uncertainty, extraction issue, feasibility problem or technical-format flag.

The competence code is an audit of demonstrated requirements, not a percentage formula. Do not mechanically map 0/0.5/1 onto grade bands.

## 6. Level 7 operational grade orientation and ceiling

- Below 50: below the Master's pass standard.
- 50–59: satisfactory postgraduate achievement with material limitations.
- 60–69: good to very good postgraduate work, normally Merit territory.
- 70–79: excellent, critically informed and independent work, normally Distinction territory.
- 80–85: rare outstanding achievement with exceptional integration, judgement, originality or publication-standard features appropriate to the poster genre.

**Tutor policy for this assignment: 85 is the absolute maximum at every marking stage.**

The routine upper ceiling is **79**. Any criterion or overall mark of **80–85** requires an explicit written exceptional-quality rationale grounded in the poster evidence and confirmed during cross-cohort calibration. There is no quota for exceptional grades.

This is an operational tutor-calibration rule; it does not redefine the University's published descriptors.

## 7. Cohort marking and calibration workflow

### Stage 1 — inventory and independent marking

Pin the repository commit and inventory every authorised poster. Preserve exact filenames/student IDs and identify missing, duplicate or unreadable submissions.

Assess all posters while blind to the desired cohort distribution. Do not use the target mean to influence evidence coding or quality judgements.

### Stage 2 — provisional criterion and overall marks

Aggregate the quality judgements into provisional marks for the six official criteria.

Calculate the independent weighted overall:

`0.20 KU + 0.20 Methodology + 0.20 Ethics + 0.20 Presentation + 0.10 Criticality + 0.10 Academic Integrity`

Retain decimals internally.

### Stage 3 — cross-cohort calibration

Compare students on the **same subcriteria**, not simply whole posters.

Review:

- borderline pass/fail cases;
- all criterion or overall marks of 80+;
- unusually high/low judgements;
- ties and near-ties;
- disagreement between competence code and quality mark;
- possible systematic examiner severity;
- cases where diagrams or concise poster design may have been under-credited.

Use earlier benchmark posters or anonymised evidence excerpts where useful. If an interpretation changes, apply it consistently to every affected submission.

Freeze the calibrated competence matrix, quality matrix, evidence notes, eligibility flags and change log before numerical normalisation.

## 8. Normed cohort moderation

The tutor sets the target cohort mean before moderation. For this cohort the target mean should be recorded explicitly before running the numerical stage. Unless another spread is specified, use **population SD = 7 as a soft target**.

Use a single common positive affine transformation of the evidence-based quality marks. Do not separately force every criterion to the same mean/distribution.

Default evidence movement is limited to at most **±5 grade points per criterion**, narrowed where the evidence requires tighter bounds.

Use 79 as the ordinary ceiling and 85 only for approved exceptional cases.

Confirmed academic failures must not be normalised into passes merely to achieve the target mean. Evidence-supported ties must not be broken by participant identity. Do not use rank-to-normal conversion, fixed proportions in grade bands, random noise or arbitrary tie-breaking.

If the desired mean/SD cannot be achieved without violating evidence, ceilings, pass/fail status or defensible ordering, preserve the academic evidence and report the nearest feasible distribution.

A roughly bell-shaped distribution is a diagnostic preference, not a release requirement.

## 9. Whole-number reconciliation and borderline rule

Released criterion percentages must be whole numbers and must reproduce the released overall exactly under the 20/20/20/20/10/10 weighting.

Round criterion grades jointly across the cohort where necessary; do not apply an unexplained hidden uplift to the overall.

For an unpenalised weighted total of 48.00–49.49, conduct the established mandatory consistency review. Where the work is confirmed to meet the overall pass standard, award 50. This convention does not apply below 48.00.

Any authorised administrative penalty is applied after academic moderation and the borderline decision, and pre-/post-penalty grades must be shown separately.

## 10. Feedback format

For each poster provide:

- student name/ID and poster title;
- final grade and classification;
- exactly three one-line strengths;
- exactly three one-line priority improvements;
- six criterion paragraphs in official order, each naming the weighting and criterion percentage;
- a concise overall judgement explaining the grade band and the single most important next step.

Aim for approximately 600–750 words and remain below 800 words unless the tutor requests a shorter batch format. Use British English. Do not expose the internal 0/0.5/1 matrix in student-facing feedback.

## 11. Release checks

Before release:

- all 24 subcriteria have evidence-based judgements;
- all six criterion marks and weights are correct;
- raw and moderated cohort statistics are available;
- the 85 ceiling and exceptional-rationale rule have been enforced;
- pass/fail and borderline cases have been reviewed;
- displayed criterion marks exactly reproduce the displayed overall;
- A3, main-text legibility and poster rendering have been checked;
- causal language matches the design;
- no poster has been judged against report-level detail expectations;
- exactly three strengths and three improvements appear in each feedback report;
- the final cohort mean and population SD are reported;
- tutor review occurs before student release.
