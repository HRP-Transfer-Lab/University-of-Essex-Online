# Biological Psychology — Syllabus and Assessments

## Module Home

#### Hi Mark! Welcome to the Biological Psychology Module.

![decorative](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1650334/course/section/293630/biop-module-home.jpg)

Biological Psychology studies our thoughts and emotions as biological phenomena. Biological Psychologists are interested in how our cognitive processes, emotions and behaviour relate to the function of the brain.

In **Unit 1**, we will cover key concepts in Biological Psychology. We will define biological psychology and explore the different levels at which we can study the biology of mind and behaviour. We will also look at how our understanding of the biology of mind and behaviour has developed over time, from the earliest philosophers to modern-day neuroscientists.

In **Unit 2**, we will look at the biology of the nervous system. The nervous system consists of the brain, spinal cord and all the nerves in the body. Together these components of the nervous system control our behaviour and provide the biological basis of our thoughts and feelings. We will study the structure and function of the nervous system, with an emphasis on the brain. We will also look at how individual neurons (nerve cells) transmit signals within the nervous system.

The focus in **Unit 3** will be the methods used in Biological Psychology. The range of methods is continually growing, as medical technology becomes increasingly sophisticated. We will look at four commonly used methods. The first will be the lesion approach, which examines the relationships between brain injury and cognitive disorders. We will also look at how we can measure activity in the brain using brain imaging and electroencephalography (EEG), and finally we will briefly cover a common brain stimulation technique. For each of the methods, we will consider their advantages and limitations in research.

**Units 4 to 7** will see us studying different cognitive processes, and what we know about the brain basis of these different processes. In **Unit 4**, we will explore the interlinked cognitive functions of sensation, perception and attention. We will look at theories of how we perceive and attend to the world. We will also consider the brain circuits important in these functions, and the disorders that can develop when these circuits are damaged.

**Unit 5** will focus on the “problem of consciousness”. This refers to the difficulty of understanding how a physical organ such as the brain can bring about mental experience. Although this is a question that neuroscience struggles to answer (and often avoids), there are some indications about how consciousness occurs in the brain that we can pick up from studying various disorders. We will investigate these conditions in Unit 5.

The focus in **Unit 6** will be learning and memory. We will learn about how changes in synapses underpin our ability to learn. We will also consider the different memory systems present in the brain, and what amnesia tells us about the biology of memory.

**Unit 7** will see us study the biology of language. We will investigate how language comes about through activity in the brain, and we will consider what goes wrong with language in brain-damaged individuals who develop an acquired language disorder (aphasia).

In **Unit 8**, we will slightly alter our focus on the brain and cognition to look at the biology of emotions. We will consider what scientists know about how the brain brings about emotional experience. We will cover different theories of emotion, and we will discuss the popular division between cognition and emotion: are thought and feeling really separate?

In the final unit, **Unit 9**, we will learn about biological explanations for psychiatric disorders, such as schizophrenia and depression. We will look at the role that neurotransmitters and genetics play in these disorders, and we will debate the importance of biological psychiatry in understanding mental health and illness.

## Module Aims and Learning Outcomes

### This module aims to develop students':

- Understanding of the biology behind thoughts, feelings and behaviour.
- Ability to evaluate the history and development of the field.
- Ability to evaluate principle approaches in Biological Psychology.
- Knowledge of current breakthroughs, arguments and debates in Biological Psychology.

### On completion of this module, you will be able to:

1. Explain relationships between the structure and function of the nervous system and human thought, emotion and behaviour.
2. Critically analyse the methods used in Biological Psychology.
3. Evaluate our understanding of the biological correlates of cognitive processes, including memory, consciousness, language, and attention.

# Assessment 1 — Units 2 and 3 Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ)

The purpose of this collaborative learning activity is a type of discussion activity which promotes higher-level thinking, communication skills, self-management and leadership skills. This is achieved through student interaction, engagement with course materials and wider reading around the module topics. This activity allows students to understand a variety of diverse perspectives on topics in psychology and helps to prepare you for real life social and employment opportunities.

After the tutor has opened the discussion with a post, students are required to offer an **initial post** that demonstrates their answer to the question, then read and respond to two of their peers' initial posts, making comments and asking questions, **always using an academic tone and backing up your ideas with APA referencing**.

From those posts, animation and leadership, application to the real world, and reflection will be assessed. The initial post should be labelled **‘Initial Post’** and the two follow-up responses should be labelled as **'Peer Response 1'** and **'Peer Response 2'**. Along with the initial post, the two responses will be judged according to all grading criteria.

## Question

**Initial post:** Using the online library to search for relevant journal articles, identify one empirical study that investigates a relationship between the structure or function of the human nervous system and human thought, emotion and/or behaviour. Briefly outline the aim, procedure and findings of the study. Clearly explain what relationship between brain and thought, emotion or behaviour is demonstrated.

**Follow-up responses:** Please respond to two posts by your fellow students, evaluating the methods we use to study the nervous system. Refer to the empirical studies mentioned in the two other posts, and discuss the strengths and weaknesses of the particular methodologies used.

## Preparation

Consider the material from Units 1 to 3: specifically, the structure and function of the nervous system, and research methods in biological psychology.

## Instructions

- Post a **500-word response** to the question above.
- Respond to two of your peers’ posts (**300 words per response**).
- Your answers must use an academic tone, be **evidence-based** and supported by the psychological literature, using **APA referencing**.
- This collaborative learning activity lasts for **two weeks**. It starts at the beginning of **Unit 2** and finishes at the end of **Unit 3**.
- Your **initial response must be posted within the first week**, and your **two follow-up responses must be posted by the end of the second week** of the activity.
- Please label your posts as Initial Response, Peer Response 1, and Peer Response 2.
- Your follow-up posts should not be standalone points; they must be clearly connected to previous posts to **provide evidence of reflection on other students’ work**.
- You should **post and comment informally** throughout the two-week period to show evidence of **animation, leadership** and to aid your learning.
- You can make a total of **five informal posts**, but the content of these contributions will not be assessed and does not count towards your overall word count.
- Remember to include your Initial post and responses (writing) within the discussion forum text box rather than attaching a Word/PDF file.
- [**See here for some tips on how you can get better grades in the CLQ activity**](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1650383/mod_forum/intro/CLQ%20Grading%20Criteria%20Tips.pdf).
- The collaborative learning question will remain open for 2 weeks.
- Any responses added after the deadline will not be assessed.
- You must submit **all three responses** to pass this assessment.
- You will be assessed on your contribution to this 2-week activity at the end of Unit 3.
- All responses must be referenced correctly using APA referencing and acknowledge all sources of information used to back up your ideas.
- This assessment cannot be blind marked due to the active participation of our tutors within the discussions.
- For students on combined programmes, it is important that you confirm your referencing style within your posts, as this may differ from the style used by other students on this module. We recommend confirming your style within the title of your reference list, for example, **'Reference List (Harvard)'**.

# Assessment 2 — Narrative Literature Review

**Due:** End of Unit 9  
**Word length:** 2,000 words, excluding references  
**Weighting:** 70%

## Instructions

This 2,000-word narrative literature review assignment gives you the opportunity to independently develop a critical synthesis of biological research in psychology. A narrative literature review allows flexibility in exploring different perspectives, theoretical approaches, and research findings, while using a structured approach to review relevant literature.

## Preparation

You are encouraged to read widely, starting from the provided key readings, and moving on to independently sourced journal articles that align with your chosen topic. Suggested databases include PubMed, PsycINFO, and Google Scholar. You should aim for a comprehensive understanding of the key concepts and research relevant to your topic area.

## Assessment Topics

Choose one of the following topics from your module units for your review:

- Biology of Sensation, Perception, and Attention
- Biology of Consciousness
- Biology of Learning and Memory
- Biology of Language
- Biology of Emotions
- Biology of Psychiatric Disorders

Each topic should allow a focus on biological processes, methodological considerations, and theoretical insights.

## Suggested Titles

For guidance, example titles include:

- “The Neurobiology of Memory: Mechanisms and Models of Memory Consolidation”.
- “Understanding Consciousness through the Biological Lens: A Narrative Review”.
- “Emotion Regulation and the Brain: Insights from Biological Psychology”.

## Suggested Structure of the Literature Review

These are subheadings you can use.

### Introduction

- Provide background and define key terms and concepts for the chosen topic.
- Present a clear research question or central theme for the review (e.g., "How does the biological basis of consciousness enhance our understanding of awareness and cognitive function?").
- State the review's aims and rationale, such as identifying major findings, gaps, or theoretical debates.

### Methodology

- Describe the literature search strategy, including databases used, search terms, and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Include any limitations on the scope of studies reviewed, such as focusing on recent research or specific methodologies.
- Consider a reflexivity statement about potential influences on study selection.

### Findings / Thematic Synthesis

- Organise findings around major themes, each with subheadings for clarity.
- Possible subheadings might include:
  - Neurobiological Mechanisms (e.g., neural circuits involved in emotion regulation)
  - Theoretical Frameworks (e.g., theories of attention in sensation and perception)
  - Empirical Evidence (key studies or experimental approaches)
- Within each section, summarise and critically evaluate studies, highlighting methodological strengths and limitations.

### Discussion and Conclusion

- Summarise key findings and address implications for practice, theory, or future research.
- Discuss any limitations of the narrative review approach used in the context of the topic.
- Conclude with reflections on how biological approaches contribute to our understanding of psychological processes.

## APA Formatting

The narrative literature review should follow APA style guidelines, including in-text citations and the reference list.

If you are following a different referencing format, please inform your tutor.

## Additional Guidance on Resources

For a deeper understanding of how to structure a narrative literature review, you are encouraged to refer to the University of Essex Online **Guide to Narrative Review Dissertations**, found in the Psychology Technical Hub. This guide offers detailed advice on structuring and conducting narrative reviews, including research questions, literature search boundaries, and reflexivity. It also provides examples and a framework for critical synthesis.

## Additional Guidance on References and Sources

You are expected to include **at least 10 scholarly references** for this literature review, with an emphasis on sourcing high-quality, peer-reviewed journal articles.

Reliable online databases such as **APA PsycInfo** and **Web of Science** are recommended for finding relevant studies and reviews. Using these databases ensures access to high-quality research sources.

For details on how to use these databases effectively, including guidance on keyword searches and refining search criteria, please refer to **Unit 7** of the module materials. This unit provides step-by-step instructions and tips to support your literature search strategy, helping you gather relevant and high-quality sources to construct a comprehensive narrative review.

## Academic Integrity and Plagiarism

**We take academic integrity very seriously.** Academic integrity means acting with fairness and honesty, giving credit to others where you are referring to their ideas or research and respecting the work of others. Plagiarism is defined as: 'Using or copying the work of others (whether written, printed or in any other form) without proper acknowledgement'.

Before you finalise your assignment take time to check that all your statements are backed up with supporting evidence, and that all sources you use — whether referring to their ideas, quoting directly or paraphrasing — are correctly referenced in the text.

Failure to properly acknowledge the work of others is an academic offence and may result in your work incurring a penalty or, in the most serious cases, you being removed from the course for academic dishonesty.

If you are unsure about referencing or plagiarism there are useful resources available in the Study Skills Hub which is accessible from the menu on the left hand side. If you are still experiencing difficulties with academic integrity then you can contact the Study Skills Team for individualised support [**studyskills-kol@kaplan.com**](mailto:studyskills-kol@kaplan.com).

# Formative Assessment — Unit 7 Literature Search Exercise

## Task Instructions

1. **Select Keywords and Databases**
   - Choose 5–10 keywords or phrases relevant to your chosen topic (e.g., “neurobiology of memory”, “consciousness and neural networks”, “emotion regulation neural mechanisms”).
   - Use databases such as **APA PsycInfo** or **Web of Science** to conduct your search.
   - To access these databases, review the information on databases in the [**UoEO Psychology Technical Hub**](https://www.my-course.co.uk/course/view.php?id=7821&section=12).

2. **Perform Initial Literature Searches**
   - Conduct searches using combinations of your keywords.
   - Try refining your search by using Boolean operators (e.g., AND, OR, NOT) to narrow or expand results.
   - Identify a **preliminary set of 3 articles** that seem most relevant to your topic.

3. **Summarise Key Findings and Search Strategy**
   - Write a brief paragraph for each selected article summarising its main focus, relevance, and any key findings or theories it presents.
   - Write a short paragraph reflecting on your search strategy, including challenges encountered, useful search terms, or observations on the breadth or specificity of the results.

4. **Post to the Discussion Thread**
   - Upload your summaries, selected articles, and reflections on your search strategy to the designated discussion thread by the deadline at the end of Unit 7.

5. **Tutor Feedback**
   - Your tutor will provide feedback on the relevance and focus of your selected articles, search strategy, and clarity of summaries.
   - They may suggest additional keywords, alternate sources, or recommend refining the scope of your topic.

## Learning Outcomes

This formative assessment will help you:

- Develop a structured, systematic approach to literature searches.
- Refine your understanding of your topic’s scope, boundaries, and terminology.
- Receive targeted feedback on your initial literature choices and research direction.

This task will prepare you to build a comprehensive, focused narrative literature review in your final assessment, and ensure that your search strategy is efficient and well-aligned with your chosen topic.
