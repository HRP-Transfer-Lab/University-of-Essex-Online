Welcome to **Unit 3 of Biological Psychology!**

Having spent Unit 2 looking at the structure and function of the nervous system, this week we turn to a crucial question: **how do we actually study relationships between the brain and behaviour?**

In Unit 3, we will examine four important approaches used in Biological Psychology: **lesion methods, brain imaging, EEG/ERP, and brain stimulation**. For each method, the aim is not simply to learn how it works, but to think critically about its **strengths, limitations, and the kinds of conclusions it allows us to draw**.

A useful question to keep asking throughout the unit is:

> **What does this method actually show, and what would go beyond the evidence?**

For example, observing activity in a brain region during a task can demonstrate an association, but it does not automatically show that the region is necessary for that psychological function. By contrast, lesion evidence can sometimes provide stronger evidence about necessity, although it comes with its own important limitations.

## Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ) – Final Week

Your **Collaborative Learning Question is ongoing and finishes at the end of Unit 3**.

By now, your **500-word Initial Response should be posted**. Your main priority this week is to complete your **two 300-word peer responses**, in which you critically evaluate the methodologies used in two classmates’ chosen empirical studies.

Remember that strong peer responses should go beyond listing generic advantages and disadvantages. Try to connect your evaluation directly to the particular study:

- What does the method measure or manipulate?
- What kind of brain–behaviour inference does it support?
- What are the most relevant strengths and limitations for this research question?
- Do the conclusions go further than the method justifies?
- Would another method provide a different or stronger form of evidence?

Please also remember that **all three assessed contributions are required**: the Initial Response, Peer Response 1 and Peer Response 2. Your posts should be evidence-based, written in an academic tone, and supported with appropriate academic referencing.

## Seminar this week

We also have our **Unit 3 seminar this week**, and it is designed to help directly with the CLQ peer responses.

A central focus will be the difference between **lesion evidence and functional neuroimaging**, particularly the distinction between evidence for **association** and evidence that a brain structure may be **necessary** for a function.

The core seminar reading is:

**Fellows, L. K., Heberlein, A. S., Morales, D. A., Shivde, G., Waller, S., & Wu, D. H. (2005). _Method matters: An empirical study of impact in cognitive neuroscience._ Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience, 17(6), 850–858.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/0898929054021139

As you read, think about this question:

> **Which method gives the stronger answer – and stronger for what kind of claim?**

A useful supplementary reading is:

**Chatterjee, A. (2005). _A madness to the methods in cognitive neuroscience?_ Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience, 17(6), 847–849.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/0898929054021085

The seminar should give you a chance to rehearse exactly the kind of methodological reasoning expected in your peer responses, so please come ready to discuss the strengths, weaknesses and inferential limits of different approaches.

Unit 3 brings together the nervous-system knowledge from Unit 2 with the critical methodological thinking that will underpin much of the rest of the module. It is also an important opportunity to finish the CLQ strongly.
