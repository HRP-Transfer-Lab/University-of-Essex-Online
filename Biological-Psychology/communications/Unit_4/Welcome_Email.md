Following our focus on research methods in Unit 3, we now turn to a central question in Biological Psychology: **how does the nervous system turn sensory input into meaningful perceptions, and how do we select what to attend to?**

## This week’s focus

We will examine three closely connected processes:

- **Sensation:** detecting sensory stimuli through receptors and transmitting this information to the brain.
- **Perception:** organising and interpreting sensory information to construct representations of the world.
- **Attention:** selecting particular information to guide our responses at a given moment.

Our focus will include **vision, hearing and somatosensation**, including touch; how the brain processes **objects and space**; and the distinction between **goal-directed attention** and **stimulus-directed attention**. As you study these topics, consider how the processes interact rather than treating them as entirely separate stages.

## Working with the evidence

Please work through the unit reading and learning materials with an eye on how biological explanations are supported by research. We will consider how **brain-imaging studies and evidence from patients with cognitive disorders** help us evaluate theories of perception and attention.

Building on Unit 3 and your CLQ work, continue to ask:

> **What does this evidence tell us about how we perceive and attend to the world, and what can it not tell us?**

The aim is not simply to identify which brain areas are involved, but to explain how sensory information is processed and to evaluate how convincingly different sources of evidence support an explanation.

You may also find it useful to note any focused questions that interest you as possible starting points for your final literature review. This unit will also provide a foundation for our next topic: **consciousness**.
