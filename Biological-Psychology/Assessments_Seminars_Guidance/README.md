## October 2026 CLQ marking clarification

Read the [CLQ summary guide](CLQ_Marking_Summary_Guide.md) alongside the detailed specification before marking this cohort. Supplied informal posts are usually **samples**, not a complete participation record. The tutor-requested **five-percentage-point adjustment** concerns confirmed absence of informal participation and requires verification, policy compatibility and a double-counting check before application. A genuinely missing assessed peer response is a more serious completion issue under the brief's **all-three-responses-required-to-pass** rule; an unclear heading or incomplete extract is not evidence of non-submission.

The guide also distinguishes the attached slides' verified content from planned seminar-reading links, records page-specific feedback examples, and clarifies the operational meaning of competence code 1. It does not replace the official rubric, introduce automatic grade release, or overwrite the detailed marking documents.

## Diagnostic scoring convention

For later marking, each subcriterion will be rated:

- **1** = fully and convincingly satisfied
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously satisfied
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

These values are diagnostic only and do not replace the official weighted rubric.

## Student-feedback voice

Feedback should sound like it comes from the tutor who taught the module.

Where relevant and supported by the seminar materials, use specific teaching links such as **“As I covered in Seminar 1…”**, **“As we discussed in Seminar 2…”**, or **“Return to the approach we practised in Seminar 3…”**. The seminar number/topic must be accurate and the reference should help the student understand or act on the feedback.

Brief encouragement is encouraged when earned, for example **“Great job here”**, **“Nicely done”**, **“Good work on this”**, **“This is a real strength”**, or **“Excellent work”**. Strong praise such as “Excellent work” should be reserved for genuinely excellent evidence.

The tone should be warm, specific, constructive and academically direct, using British English.

## Student-feedback wording conventions

These are hard style rules for student-facing feedback generated under this specification:

- **Never use “scholarly”.** Prefer **“academic”**, **“high-quality academic”**, **“research literature”**, or **“high-quality sources”**.
- Prefer natural contractions: **“don't”, “won't”, “can't”, “isn't”, “doesn't”, “you've”, “you're”** rather than unnecessarily formal uncontracted equivalents. Keep the tone professional but conversational.
- **Never use “designated textbook”.** Use **“module textbook”**.
- Apply these conventions to strengths, improvement points, criterion paragraphs and closing comments.
