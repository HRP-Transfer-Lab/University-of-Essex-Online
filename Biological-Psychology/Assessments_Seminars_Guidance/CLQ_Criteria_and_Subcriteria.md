# Biological Psychology CLQ — Criteria and Diagnostic Subcriteria

## Assessment

**Units 2–3 Collaborative Learning Question**

Students submit:

- one **500-word initial post** identifying and explaining one empirical human study linking nervous-system structure/function with thought, emotion and/or behaviour;
- two **300-word peer responses** evaluating the methods used in peers' selected studies;
- required informal participation/animation across the two-week activity as described in the assessment brief.

All three assessed responses are required to pass.

## Official criteria and weightings

1. **Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues — 30%**
2. **Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%**
3. **Communication — 20%**
4. **Reading and Referencing — 25%**

The official criteria and weightings remain unchanged. The four subcriteria below are diagnostic prompts.

---

# 1. Knowledge and Understanding — 30%

## 1.1 Accurate understanding of the selected empirical study
The student accurately identifies the study's research question/aim, participants, procedure, principal biological measure or manipulation, and main findings.

## 1.2 Accurate explanation of the brain–behaviour relationship
The student clearly explains the demonstrated relationship between nervous-system structure/function and the relevant thought, emotion or behaviour, without confusing the biological measure with the psychological construct.

## 1.3 Understanding of the research methodology
The student demonstrates accurate understanding of what the study's method measures or manipulates, including relevant features of imaging, lesion, electrophysiological, stimulation, psychophysiological or other biological methods.

## 1.4 Understanding of inferential limits and course concepts
The student connects the study to relevant module concepts and recognises what kind of inference the design supports. Strong work should distinguish, where relevant, association, prediction, localisation, necessity and causation, using seminar-led methodological scholarship such as Poldrack & Wagner (2004), Henson (2005), Fellows et al. (2005) or Chatterjee (2005).

---

# 2. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%

## 2.1 Evaluation of methodological strengths
The student identifies specific strengths of the method/design and explains why those strengths matter for the study's research question, rather than giving generic method advantages.

## 2.2 Evaluation of methodological limitations
The student identifies specific limitations, confounds or interpretive constraints and explains how they affect confidence in the study's conclusions.

## 2.3 Evaluation of inference from brain to behaviour
The student critically evaluates whether the authors' conclusions go beyond what the method can justify, including issues such as reverse inference, correlational evidence, necessity claims, localisation versus distributed networks, or generalisability where relevant.

## 2.4 Comparative critical engagement in peer responses
The peer responses engage directly with the two classmates' empirical studies and methods, using evidence to compare strengths, weaknesses and inferential value rather than offering generic comments or praise.

---

# 3. Communication — 20%

## 3.1 Clear and coherent initial post
The initial post presents the aim, procedure, findings and brain–behaviour relationship in a concise, logically organised way appropriate to the 500-word format.

## 3.2 Peer responses are directly connected and reflective
Each peer response clearly responds to the specific study and claims in the preceding post, demonstrating reflection rather than functioning as a standalone mini-essay.

## 3.3 Constructive academic dialogue and leadership
Across assessed and informal participation, the student communicates respectfully, asks useful questions, clarifies methodological issues, advances discussion and where appropriate shows animation/leadership.

## 3.4 Audience-appropriate scientific communication
Terminology is accurate, complexity is explained clearly, claims are appropriately qualified, and the student communicates biological methods without unnecessary jargon or oversimplification.

---

# 4. Reading and Referencing — 25%

## 4.1 Quality and appropriateness of the primary empirical study
The selected study is genuinely empirical, human, relevant to nervous-system structure/function and suitable for answering the CLQ rather than being a review, commentary or weakly related source.

## 4.2 Substantive engagement with seminar/module methods scholarship
The student uses relevant module/seminar readings to reason about methodology and inference. Strong work should normally engage substantively with at least one appropriate methods source such as Poldrack & Wagner (2004), Henson (2005), Fellows et al. (2005) or Chatterjee (2005). A token citation does not count.

## 4.3 Wider scholarly evidence and evidence-to-claim alignment
Additional peer-reviewed sources are relevant and actually support the methodological or substantive claims for which they are cited.

## 4.4 Accurate and consistent referencing
In-text citations and references are complete and consistent with the student's declared referencing style. APA should be used unless the student's programme requires another authorised style.

---

# Reading-engagement rule

For a **2:1 or above**, there should normally be identifiable engagement with relevant module/seminar scholarship in addition to the chosen empirical study.

For a **First**, the student should normally integrate:

- the empirical study;
- relevant methodological module reading;
- wider evidence;

into a coherent argument about what the study does and does not show.

The following do not count as substantive engagement:

- a source present only in the reference list;
- a token citation attached to a generic statement;
- repeating a methods checklist without linking it to the selected study;
- asserting that a method is “good” or “bad” without explaining the inferential consequence.

---

# Diagnostic use

Rate each subcriterion:

- **1** = fully and convincingly demonstrated
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously demonstrated
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

These ratings support, but do not mechanically determine, the official criterion percentage.
