# Biological Psychology CLQ Grading Specification

Version 1.0 · 10 October 2026

**Assessment:** Units 2–3 Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ): 500-word initial empirical-study post plus two 300-word peer responses.

**Status:** Assessment-specific configuration of the repository-level `GRADING_FEEDBACK_ENGINE_SPEC.md`. The official University of Essex Online CLQ rubric and assessment brief remain authoritative. This document operationalises them for consistent cohort marking.

## 3. Diagnostic subcriteria

### C1. Knowledge and Understanding — 30%

- **C1.1** Accurate understanding of the selected empirical study
- **C1.2** Accurate explanation of the brain–behaviour relationship
- **C1.3** Understanding of the research methodology
- **C1.4** Understanding of inferential limits and course concepts

### C2. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%

- **C2.1** Evaluation of methodological strengths
- **C2.2** Evaluation of methodological limitations
- **C2.3** Evaluation of inference from brain to behaviour
- **C2.4** Comparative critical engagement in peer responses

### C3. Communication — 20%

- **C3.1** Clear and coherent initial post
- **C3.2** Peer responses are directly connected and reflective
- **C3.3** Constructive academic dialogue and leadership
- **C3.4** Audience-appropriate scientific communication

### C4. Reading and Referencing — 25%

- **C4.1** Quality and appropriateness of the primary empirical study
- **C4.2** Substantive engagement with seminar/module methods scholarship
- **C4.3** Wider academic evidence and evidence-to-claim alignment
- **C4.4** Accurate and consistent referencing

## 4. Competence coding

Score every subcriterion independently before cohort moderation.

| Code | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Not demonstrated, fundamentally incorrect, irrelevant, or absent where required |
| **0.5** | Partly demonstrated but materially incomplete, inaccurate, weakly justified or uneven |
| **1** | Adequately and substantively demonstrated at Level 5; minor weaknesses may remain |

Each code must be accompanied by a concise evidence note and location.

These competence codes are diagnostic and are **not** converted mechanically into percentage marks.

---

## 5. Independent subcriterion quality marks

Assign a separate quality mark `q_icj` to each subcriterion after the 0/0.5/1 competence judgement.

### 5.1 Normal operating range

For this assessment, **subcriterion quality marks should normally fall between 45 and 85**.

This is a tutor calibration policy, not a University grade-band rule.

Typical interpretation:

| Quality mark | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| **80–85** | Rare exceptional performance; unusually strong methodological reasoning, synthesis or scientific communication |
| **70–79** | Strong first-class performance; accurate, critical, independent and well supported |
| **60–69** | Good 2:1 performance; competent and developed with identifiable limitations |
| **50–59** | Adequate 2:2 performance; relevant but limited, uneven or insufficiently critical |
| **45–49** | Weak but meaningful evidence; substantial limitations |
| **40–44** | Borderline / marginal evidence; substantive weaknesses requiring close review |
| **Below 40** | Dramatically weak, absent or seriously flawed performance; mandatory tutor-review flag |

### 5.2 Relationship between competence code and quality mark

- **Code 1:** normally supports about 55–85, depending on depth, precision and sophistication.
- **Code 0.5:** normally supports about 45–59. A quality mark of 60+ requires a clear written rationale.
- **Code 0:** normally supports 0–44 depending on whether some relevant but non-demonstrative material exists. A wholly absent subcriterion should normally receive 0.

Do not force dramatically weak work into the 45–85 range.

### 5.3 Mandatory review flags

Flag for tutor review where:

- any subcriterion quality mark is **below 40**;
- any official criterion quality mark is **below 40**;
- the independent or processed overall is **below 40**;
- there are multiple competence codes of 0 within one official criterion;
- a required assessed response is missing;
- evidence is inaccessible or unresolved;
- the selected “empirical study” does not satisfy the assessment requirement;
- any proposed overall mark is 80–85.

---

## 5.4 Module-reading engagement rule

The CLQ should demonstrate engagement with Biological Psychology course learning, not merely selection of a plausible neuroscience paper.

Strong work should normally combine:

1. the student's selected **primary empirical human study**;
2. at least one relevant **module/seminar methods source**, especially Poldrack & Wagner (2004), Henson (2005), Fellows et al. (2005), or Chatterjee (2005);
3. appropriate wider academic evidence where needed.

A source counts only when its concepts, findings, methods or inferential limits are used in the student's reasoning.

**Band guidance:**

- **60+**: normally requires meaningful engagement with relevant module/seminar scholarship as well as the primary study;
- **70+**: normally requires integration of empirical-study detail, methods scholarship and wider evidence;
- **80–85**: requires unusually strong methodological synthesis and precision about what the evidence does and does not establish.

If there is **no meaningful engagement with identifiable module/seminar reading**, normally:

- C4.2 cannot score 1;
- C1.4 should be constrained;
- Reading and Referencing should not normally enter the First-class band.

Do not enforce a mechanical citation count.

## 6. Scientific-inference rule

A central learning outcome of this CLQ is methodological reasoning.

When marking, distinguish explicitly between:

- **association** — two measures vary together;
- **prediction** — one measure statistically predicts another;
- **localisation** — a process is associated with activity/damage in a region or network;
- **necessity** — disruption/damage to a structure impairs a function;
- **causation** — manipulation of a biological variable produces a change in thought, emotion or behaviour.

Students should not be rewarded for converting correlational evidence into causal claims.

Relevant examples:

- fMRI activation does not by itself show that a region is necessary for a function;
- lesion evidence may support necessity more strongly, but lesion extent, reorganisation and pre-existing differences limit inference;
- EEG/ERP provides strong temporal information but weak anatomical localisation;
- psychophysiological measures may be sensitive but not psychologically specific;
- stimulation methods can strengthen causal inference but still require careful interpretation.

This rule primarily informs C1.3–C1.4 and C2.1–C2.3.

## 7. Required components and eligibility

The assessed submission consists of:

1. **Initial Response** — 500 words;
2. **Peer Response 1** — 300 words;
3. **Peer Response 2** — 300 words.

The brief states that **all three assessed responses must be submitted to pass**.

Therefore:

- missing initial response: hold and flag for tutor review;
- missing either peer response: not eligible for automatic borderline uplift; hold and flag;
- missing both peer responses: substantive non-completion; hold and flag;
- inaccessible material is `unresolved`, not automatically zero, until checked.

Students may also make up to five informal posts. Their **content is not assessed** and does not count towards the word limit. Informal participation may provide contextual evidence of animation/leadership, but it cannot replace the three assessed responses and should not be graded as additional academic content.

---

## 8. Primary-study eligibility check

Before marking the initial post, verify that the chosen source is:

- an **empirical study** rather than a review/commentary;
- based on **human participants**;
- concerned with structure or function of the human nervous system;
- linked to thought, emotion and/or behaviour;
- sufficiently described for the student to explain aim, procedure and findings.

If eligibility is ambiguous:

1. inspect the original source;
2. flag uncertainty;
3. do not silently substitute a different paper.

## 9. Independent criterion and overall marks

For student `i`, criterion `c`, subcriterion `j`:

```text
X_ic = mean(x_icj)
Q_ic = mean(q_icj)
B_i  = sum_c(w_c * Q_ic)
```

Official weights:

```text
w_C1 = 0.30
w_C2 = 0.25
w_C3 = 0.20
w_C4 = 0.25
```

`B_i` is the academic-quality basis for cohort moderation. The competence matrix remains the audit trail.

## 10. Cohort calibration targets

### 10.1 Target mean

```text
target_mean = 65
```

The target applies to the complete academically eligible cohort before any administrative word-count penalty.

### 10.2 Desired spread

```text
soft_target_population_sd = 8
typical_overall_range ≈ 45–85
```

This is a soft calibration target, not a quota.

Do not:

- force a Gaussian distribution;
- manufacture failures;
- manufacture 80+ marks;
- clip genuinely poor work upward to 45.

### 10.3 Moderation bounds

- preserve meaningful ties;
- preserve rank ordering where evidence is comparable;
- use a common constrained affine transformation following the repository engine;
- criterion movement from independent quality is normally limited to **±5 points**.

## 11. Borderline-fail policy

The undergraduate pass threshold is 40.

Tutor moderation convention:

```text
if processed_overall in [38, 39]
and all three assessed responses are present
and the selected primary study is eligible
and no essential criterion is wholly absent
and no unresolved evidence remains:
    academic_overall = 40
    record borderline_uplift = true
else:
    retain evidence-based result or hold for review
```

Do not uplift merely to achieve the target mean.

### 11.1 Extreme / substantive failures

Any processed overall **below 38** is not finalised automatically.

Required action:

1. verify all assessed posts were retrieved correctly;
2. re-check all 16 subcriteria;
3. verify the selected study and references;
4. check for extraction/display problems;
5. record the reason for the fail;
6. flag for tutor review.

The tutor decides the final mark.

---

## 12. Exceptional grades

- routine upper bound: **79**
- exceptional range: **80–85**
- absolute maximum: **85**

Any 80–85 criterion or overall requires an evidence-based rationale demonstrating unusually strong Level 5 performance.

For this CLQ, exceptional work would normally show unusually precise methodological reasoning, substantive integration of module scholarship, and sophisticated peer critique within the short word limits.

---

## 13. Moderation procedure

1. Score all **16 subcriteria** using 0/0.5/1 without reference to cohort targets.
2. Assign independent quality marks to all 16 subcriteria.
3. Aggregate to four independent criterion marks and an independent weighted overall.
4. Complete a cross-cohort calibration pass on the same subcriteria.
5. Review:
   - all subcriteria below 40;
   - all criterion marks below 40;
   - all fail candidates;
   - all 80+ candidates;
   - missing assessed responses;
   - ineligible/ambiguous empirical studies;
   - inconsistent competence/quality combinations.
6. Freeze evidence-based marks.
7. Apply constrained cohort moderation towards mean 65 and population SD 8, subject to evidence bounds.
8. Apply 38–39 → 40 only to eligible cases.
9. Hold all remaining fail cases for tutor review.
10. Jointly round criterion marks so displayed weighted criteria reproduce the academic overall as closely as feasible.
11. Report achieved mean, SD, minimum, maximum and all review flags.

## 14. Word-count policy

The brief specifies:

- Initial Response: **500 words**
- Peer Response 1: **300 words**
- Peer Response 2: **300 words**

A word-count penalty applies if the authorised limit/range is exceeded by **more than 10%**, producing a **10 percentage-point reduction**.

Potential numerical thresholds are:

```text
initial_response_threshold = 550
peer_response_threshold = 330 each
```

However, before applying a penalty, verify whether the institutional rule is applied **separately to each assessed post or to the assessed contribution collectively**. The brief does not resolve this operational detail.

If unclear near the threshold:

- flag the case;
- do not invent a counting convention.

Apply any authorised penalty **after** academic moderation and do not renormalise afterwards.

No automatic deduction applies for under-length work.

---

## 15. Referencing-style rule

APA is the default stated style.

For students on combined programmes, the brief allows an authorised alternative departmental style if clearly identified. Therefore:

- do not penalise an authorised alternative style merely for not being APA;
- assess consistency and accuracy within the declared style;
- flag unclear cases rather than assuming misconduct.

## 16. Feedback format

For each student:

- exactly **3 strengths**;
- exactly **3 priority improvements**;
- one paragraph for each of the **4 official criteria**;
- official criterion percentages only;
- academic overall and final grade if a penalty changes it;
- two-sentence personalised closing observation.


### Feedback voice and seminar-linking

Student-facing feedback should sound like feedback from the tutor who has taught the module, not a detached rubric summary.

Where it is genuinely relevant to the student's work, make **specific links back to the taught seminars**, for example:

- “As we covered in Seminar 1, …”
- “This connects well with the point we discussed in Seminar 2 about …”
- “For the next assignment, return to the approach we practised in Seminar 3 …”

Rules:

- use the **actual seminar number and topic** supported by the module's seminar materials / `Seminar_Led_Reading_Integration.md`;
- make the seminar reference analytically useful — it should remind the student of a concept, method, reading or activity that helps explain the feedback;
- do not force a seminar reference into every criterion paragraph;
- normally include **at least one useful seminar connection in the overall feedback when an appropriate connection exists**;
- never claim something was taught in a seminar if the source materials do not support that claim.

Use brief, natural encouragement where warranted. Appropriate phrases include:

- “Great job here.”
- “Excellent work.”
- “Nicely done.”
- “This is a real strength.”
- “Good work on this.”
- “You handled this well.”
- “This is developing well.”

Praise must be **evidence-calibrated**:

- reserve “Excellent work” and similarly strong praise for genuinely excellent / First-class evidence;
- use warmer but more measured phrases such as “Nicely done” or “Good work on this” for solid achievement;
- avoid generic praise that is not tied to something the student actually did;
- do not make every paragraph begin or end with praise.

The overall tone should be **warm, encouraging, specific and academically direct**. Areas for improvement should be framed constructively and, where possible, tell the student what to do next rather than merely naming a weakness.

### Required wording conventions in student feedback

These are hard style rules for all student-facing feedback:

- **Never use “scholarly”.** Use **“academic”**, **“high-quality academic”**, **“research literature”**, or **“high-quality sources”** as appropriate.
- Use natural contractions in feedback: **“don't”, “won't”, “can't”, “isn't”, “doesn't”, “you've”, “you're”** rather than unnecessarily formal **“do not”, “will not”, “cannot”, “is not”, “does not”, “you have”, “you are”**. Keep the tone professional, but conversational.
- **Never use “designated textbook”.** Refer to it as the **“module textbook”**.
- These wording rules apply to headings, criterion paragraphs, strengths, improvement points and the closing observation.


Do not show students:

- diagnostic 0/0.5/1 codes;
- subcriterion quality marks;
- cohort-normalisation calculations;
- internal tutor-review flags.

Use British English.

## 19. Release gates

No grade is released until:

- all three assessed responses are accounted for;
- the primary empirical study has been checked for eligibility;
- all 16 subcriteria are scored or explicitly unresolved;
- every sub-40 subcriterion has been reviewed;
- every fail candidate has been checked;
- every extreme-fail candidate has tutor approval;
- every 80–85 result has a rationale;
- any word-count penalty has been independently verified;
- displayed criterion marks reproduce the released overall;
- cohort statistics are reported honestly;
- feedback matches the final grade-sheet run.
