## 2. What is assessed

The submission comprises a **500-word initial response**, **Peer Response 1 (300 words)** and **Peer Response 2 (300 words)**. The initial response must identify an empirical human study relating nervous-system structure/function to thought, emotion and/or behaviour, outline its aim, procedure and findings, and explain the brain–behaviour relationship. Each peer response must evaluate the methodology of a classmate's selected study and connect directly to that post.

**All three assessed responses are required to pass.** Check the whole submission and, where needed, the original forum before classifying anything as absent. A review/commentary or a non-human study does not satisfy the primary-study requirement. Animal examples used in teaching do not waive this requirement.

Informal posts provide contextual evidence of participation, animation and leadership. Their academic content is not an additional assessed essay and does not enter the assessed word count. They cannot automatically substitute for a required peer response.

## 3. Criteria and diagnostic subcriteria

| Criterion | Weight | Four diagnostic subcriteria |
|---|---:|---|
| **C1. Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues** | **30%** | **C1.1** Accurate study understanding: aim, participants, procedure, biological measure/manipulation and findings. **C1.2** Accurate brain–behaviour explanation. **C1.3** Understanding of what the method measures/manipulates. **C1.4** Inferential limits and relevant course concepts. |
| **C2. Critical Analysis and Evaluation** | **25%** | **C2.1** Study-specific methodological strengths. **C2.2** Study-specific limitations and their consequences. **C2.3** Evaluation of the brain-to-behaviour inference. **C2.4** Direct, evidence-based critical engagement with both peers' studies. |
| **C3. Communication** | **20%** | **C3.1** Clear, coherent and concise initial post. **C3.2** Connected, reflective peer responses. **C3.3** Constructive academic dialogue and leadership. **C3.4** Accurate, accessible and appropriately qualified scientific communication. |
| **C4. Reading and Referencing** | **25%** | **C4.1** Suitable primary empirical human study. **C4.2** Substantive use of relevant module/seminar methods literature. **C4.3** Relevant wider academic evidence supporting the claims made. **C4.4** Accurate, consistent citations and references. |

### Evidence first; quality second

For every subcriterion, record a competence code, a concise evidence note and its location, then a **separate independent quality mark**.

| Code | Operational meaning |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Not demonstrated, fundamentally incorrect, irrelevant, or genuinely absent where required. |
| **0.5** | Partly demonstrated, but materially incomplete, inaccurate, weakly justified or uneven. |
| **1** | Adequately and substantively demonstrated at Level 5; minor weaknesses may remain. |

Use the operational definition above from the grading specification. The criteria document's wording “fully and convincingly demonstrated” must not be interpreted as requiring exceptional quality for code 1. This wording difference is recorded here; the underlying source files have not been silently rewritten.

Codes are **not percentages** and must not be multiplied into quality marks. Inaccessible or ambiguous evidence is `unresolved`, not automatically zero. Quality marks normally fall within 45–85, but genuinely absent/very weak evidence must not be forced up to 45. Sub-40 judgements require review; 80–85 requires an exceptional-quality rationale. A competence code of 1 does not automatically imply First-class work.

Average the four quality marks within each criterion, then calculate:

```text
Independent overall = 0.30*C1 + 0.25*C2 + 0.20*C3 + 0.25*C4
```

### What strong academic evaluation looks like

Distinguish **association, prediction, localisation, necessity and causation**. Reward an explanation of why a methodological strength or limitation matters for the particular study's conclusions, not a generic advantages/disadvantages checklist. Judge both peer responses, not just the strongest one.

The existing specification normally expects meaningful engagement with relevant module/seminar reading for 60+, and integration of empirical detail, methods literature and wider evidence for 70+. Relevant sources include Poldrack and Wagner (2004), Henson (2005), Fellows et al. (2005) and Chatterjee (2005). These are not a compulsory four-paper checklist. A bibliography entry or token citation does not establish engagement; credit relevant concepts actually used in the argument. Do not turn normal band guidance into an undisclosed automatic overall cap.

## 4. Informal participation: samples and the requested five-point adjustment

### A supplied informal post is a sample

The tutor has usually included **one informal post per student**, although more may exist in the forum. Treat that item as a **sample of informal participation**, not as the complete participation history.

- Use wording such as **“In the sample informal post provided…”** or **“Your sample informal contribution shows…”**.
- Do not write “you only made one informal post” or infer frequency, timing across the fortnight, or sustained leadership from a single sample.
- Do not penalise a student because only one sample was selected for the marking pack.
- A sample can evidence constructive interaction, but its mere presence does not automatically demonstrate high-quality leadership.

### Distinguish three situations

| Status | Action |
|---|---|
| **Sample present** | Recognise the evidenced participation; no absence deduction. Evaluate only what the sample supports. |
| **No sample supplied; wider participation unknown** | Record `informal_status = unresolved`. Check the full forum or obtain the tutor's explicit confirmation. Do not infer non-participation from an incomplete extract. |
| **No informal posts confirmed** | Record the verification source and the tutor-requested **−5 percentage-point overall adjustment**, subject to the policy/authority check in section 1. |

The requested adjustment is **five percentage points**, not 5% of the mark. Apply an authorised adjustment once, after academic moderation, and show it separately from the academic-quality overall. For example, an academic overall of 65 becomes 60 after a five-point adjustment; this is an illustration, not a student grade.

**Avoid double counting.** Assess C3.3 from the actual dialogue/leadership evidence, including assessed responses. Do not automatically zero C3.3 or add another discretionary criterion penalty solely because informal participation is absent, then deduct five more points for the same absence. Record any genuine qualitative weakness separately. If the official assessment arrangements require participation to be handled wholly within Communication, use that authorised route instead of stacking a new overall deduction.

Do not assess the informal post as an extra academic mini-essay. The adjustment concerns verified absence of required participation, not the scientific sophistication of an informal comment.

## 5. Peer Response 2: unclear is not the same as missing

A missing heading is not proof of a missing assessed response. Check the complete document and forum context for a **distinct second response to another classmate's study**, including unlabelled or mislabelled text. Identify its location, recipient/study, assessment status and relevant submission timing. Do not count the same response twice or automatically relabel an informal sample as an assessed response.

| Finding | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Second assessed response present but mislabelled** | Assess its substance. Any genuine clarity/presentation weakness belongs in Communication; do not impose a missing-response penalty. |
| **Second assessed response present but weak, short or insufficiently methodological** | Mark the demonstrated quality against the criteria. Weakness is not automatically non-submission; there is no automatic under-length deduction. |
| **Second response not identifiable in the available extract** | Record `peer_response_2_status = unresolved`; verify the full record before deciding. Do not apply an absence penalty merely for uncertainty. |
| **Second assessed response genuinely absent after verification** | Treat as substantive non-completion: **not eligible for a pass under the brief's all-three-responses rule**, no borderline uplift, and mandatory tutor review. |

**The consequence for a confirmed omission is more substantial than the five-point participation adjustment.** Do not substitute an arbitrary −10 or −15 deduction that could still produce a passing grade despite non-completion. Use the University's authorised non-completion outcome. Where a numerical fail is the appropriate outcome, **39 is the highest mark compatible with a 40 pass threshold**, not an automatic award of 39 and not a newly discovered institutional capping rule. The exact result must be authorised by the tutor under the applicable rules.

Retain an evidence-based academic-quality assessment of the available work and a separate completion decision. Do not invent blanket zeros across all four criteria. Do not add another flat penalty for the same missing response on top of a non-completion outcome unless an applicable policy explicitly requires it. The same verification and review principles apply to a missing initial response or Peer Response 1.

## 6. Moderation, deductions and release order

Mark independently before looking at cohort targets; then compare performance on the same subcriteria across students. The existing local configuration is **target mean 65**, **soft population SD 8**, criterion movement normally within **±5 points**, routine ceiling **79**, and exceptional range **80–85** with written justification. These are tutor settings, not University grade-band rules. Preserve meaningful ties and evidence-based distinctions; do not manufacture a normal distribution, passes, failures or exceptional marks.

Use this sequence:

1. Verify components, primary-study eligibility and evidence access; record unresolved cases.
2. Score all 16 subcriteria, add quality marks and evidence notes, and aggregate the four weighted criteria.
3. Cross-check cohort calibration and review failures, low subcriteria and exceptional candidates.
4. Apply permitted academic moderation within evidence bounds. The existing 38–39 → 40 convention applies only to otherwise eligible, complete submissions, never to a missing-response case.
5. Determine the authorised completion outcome; hold unresolved or non-complete cases for tutor review.
6. For eligible complete cases, apply any verified and permitted participation and word-count adjustments separately, after academic moderation. Do not renormalise afterwards or use a later uplift to erase a deduction.
7. Reconcile displayed criterion marks with the academic overall, then show the adjustment arithmetic leading to the final grade. Explain any completion outcome separately.

For a complete, eligible case with authorised deductions only:

```text
Final grade = max(0, academic overall - participation deduction - word-count deduction)
```

A held case has **no released final grade** until its material questions are resolved. Report the cohort denominator, exclusions/holds and both academic and final-grade statistics transparently.

### Word count and referencing

The existing brief specifies a **10-percentage-point deduction** for exceeding the authorised word limit/range by **more than 10%**. It does not settle whether this is applied to each response separately or collectively. **550 words initially and 330 per peer response are potential per-post thresholds only.** Verify counting inclusions/exclusions and the applicable convention before imposing a penalty; do not silently stack separate penalties. No automatic deduction applies to under-length work.

APA is the default. An authorised alternative style for a combined programme is acceptable when identified; assess accuracy and consistency within it.

Normally include at least one useful connection where appropriate, not a compulsory seminar reference in every paragraph. Do not imply a particular student's attendance or contribution unless documented.

### Adaptable examples, not boilerplate

> “As we covered in Seminar 1, activation can support an inference about involvement without uniquely identifying a mental process. Clarify what the study measured and consider the alternative interpretation before concluding that the activity demonstrates fear.”

> “As we covered in Seminar 1, the value of a method depends on the claim being tested. Explain why the EEG timing is useful for this study's question, while acknowledging what it can't establish about anatomical localisation.”

> “As we covered in Seminar 2, regional specialisation doesn't mean that one area works alone. Explain how this region contributes to a wider network rather than describing it as the sole location of the function.”

> “As we covered in Seminar 2, functional coupling describes statistical co-variation. It doesn't by itself establish a direct anatomical connection or the direction of influence, so qualify that conclusion.”

> “As we covered in Seminar 2, changes in synaptic strength and structural reorganisation are different aspects of plasticity. Specify which kind of change the study actually measured.”

Only use an example where the student's study and writing warrant it. Do not invent a misconception, a seminar contribution or a strength to fit an example. The connections above preserve the supplied slides' concepts without adding contemporary-study claims that would require separate verification.

## 8. Required student-feedback format

Provide **exactly three strengths**, **exactly three priority improvements**, **one paragraph for each of the four official criteria with its percentage**, the academic overall and any separately explained adjustment/final grade, and a **two-sentence personalised closing observation**.

Be warm, encouraging, specific and academically direct. Tie praise to real evidence and reserve stronger praise for genuinely excellent performance. Give actionable next steps. Use British English, natural contractions and the terms **“academic”** and **“module textbook”**, following the wording rules in the main specification.

Mention informal evidence as a **sample**. Where absence has not been verified, write internally “no informal sample supplied; participation unverified”, not student-facing criticism alleging non-participation. Where a deduction is verified and authorised, explain its reason and amount plainly; do not hide it in the criterion prose.

Students see the main criterion percentages and a transparent grade calculation, **not** diagnostic codes, subcriterion quality marks, cohort-normalisation calculations or internal review tags. A genuine completion issue must still be communicated clearly; hiding internal flags is not a reason to hide the reason for a grade.

## 9. Internal audit and final checks

Alongside the existing 16 codes, 16 quality marks, evidence locations, criterion totals and moderation record, retain:

- informal status (`sample_present`, `unresolved`, `confirmed_absent`), sample location and any absence-verification source;
- requested participation adjustment (5 points), policy-authorisation status, actual applied adjustment and double-counting check;
- each assessed response's location and status; recipient/study identification for both peer responses; original-forum checks where needed;
- completion decision, its source, tutor approval and any authorised cap/outcome;
- verified word-count convention, count and deduction; academic overall, each adjustment, and final grade or hold status;
- seminar number/page supporting each teaching reference used in feedback.

Before release, resolve every material evidence gap, review every fail and exceptional candidate, verify any deduction, reconcile the displayed arithmetic, and ensure the feedback matches the same final grade-sheet version. No grades are automatically released by this guide.
