# Biological Psychology Narrative Literature Review Grading Specification

Version 1.0 · 10 October 2026

**Assessment:** Unit 9 Narrative Literature Review.

**Word count:** 2,000 words excluding references.  
**Assessment weighting:** 70%.  
**Minimum scholarly references:** 10.

**Status:** Assessment-specific configuration of the repository-level `GRADING_FEEDBACK_ENGINE_SPEC.md`. The official University of Essex Online Level 5 rubric and assessment brief remain authoritative.

## 2. Official criteria and weightings

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues | 25% |
| Application of Theory to Practice and/or Real-world Example | 25% |
| Critical Analysis and Evaluation | 25% |

Each criterion has exactly four diagnostic subcriteria. Within a criterion, the four subcriteria are equally weighted for diagnostic aggregation only.

## 3. Diagnostic subcriteria

### C1. Knowledge and Understanding — 25%

- **C1.1** Knowledge of the selected biological-psychology topic
- **C1.2** Understanding of major theories and conceptual debates
- **C1.3** Understanding of biological levels of analysis
- **C1.4** Understanding of methods and what they can establish

### C2. Application of Theory to Practice / Real-world Example — 25%

- **C2.1** Application of biological theory to empirical evidence
- **C2.2** Application across levels of analysis
- **C2.3** Application of methodological knowledge to substantive claims
- **C2.4** Application to real-world, clinical or functional implications where appropriate

### C3. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%

- **C3.1** Critical evaluation of theories and biological explanations
- **C3.2** Critical evaluation of empirical methods and evidence
- **C3.3** Comparative thematic synthesis
- **C3.4** Judgement, uncertainty and research gaps

### C4. Reading and Referencing — 15%

- **C4.1** Substantive engagement with module/seminar scholarship
- **C4.2** Breadth, quality and independence of the literature base
- **C4.3** Transparent and appropriate literature-search process
- **C4.4** Evidence-to-claim alignment and reference accuracy

### C5. Presentation Style and Structure — 10%

- **C5.1** Appropriate narrative-review structure
- **C5.2** Thematic rather than study-by-study organisation
- **C5.3** Coherence, academic style and conceptual precision
- **C5.4** Technical presentation and assessment requirements

## 4. Competence coding

Score every subcriterion independently before cohort moderation.

| Code | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Not demonstrated, fundamentally incorrect, irrelevant, or absent where required |
| **0.5** | Partly demonstrated but materially incomplete, inaccurate, weakly justified or uneven |
| **1** | Adequately and substantively demonstrated at Level 5; minor weaknesses may remain |

Each code must be accompanied by a concise evidence note and location.

The codes are diagnostic and are **not** converted mechanically into percentage marks.

---

## 5. Independent subcriterion quality marks

Assign a separate quality mark `q_icj` after the competence code.

### 5.1 Normal operating range

**Subcriterion quality marks should normally fall between 45 and 85.**

Typical interpretation:

| Quality mark | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| **80–85** | Rare exceptional performance; unusually strong synthesis, methodological critique and independent judgement |
| **70–79** | Strong first-class performance; developed, critical and well integrated |
| **60–69** | Good 2:1 performance; competent, analytical and well supported with identifiable limitations |
| **50–59** | Adequate 2:2 performance; relevant but limited, uneven or overly descriptive |
| **45–49** | Weak but meaningful evidence; substantial limitations |
| **40–44** | Borderline / marginal evidence requiring close review |
| **Below 40** | Dramatically weak, absent or seriously flawed performance; mandatory tutor-review flag |

### 5.2 Relationship between competence and quality

- **Code 1:** normally supports about 55–85.
- **Code 0.5:** normally supports about 45–59; 60+ requires clear written rationale.
- **Code 0:** normally supports 0–44. A wholly absent element should normally receive 0.

Very low values are permitted when academically justified and must be flagged.

### 5.3 Mandatory flags

Flag for tutor review where:

- any subcriterion quality mark is below 40;
- any criterion quality mark is below 40;
- independent or processed overall is below 40;
- multiple zeros occur within one criterion;
- an essential review component is absent;
- the literature-search methodology cannot be established;
- fewer than 10 scholarly references are present;
- evidence is inaccessible or unresolved;
- any proposed overall is 80–85.

## 5.4 Module-reading engagement rule

The review must demonstrate engagement with the Biological Psychology module as well as independent research.

### Review-method anchors

Strong work should normally demonstrate substantive understanding of narrative-review methodology using:

- **Snyder (2019)** and/or
- **Sukhera (2022)**,

alongside the University narrative-review guidance where available.

### Topic-specific anchors

Students should also use relevant module scholarship for the selected topic. Examples:

- **Consciousness:** Rees et al. (2002), Gazzaniga (2005), Zeman (2001)
- **Learning & memory:** Eichenbaum (2011) plus relevant H.M./amnesia evidence
- **Language:** Price (2012), Friederici (2011)
- **Emotion:** LeDoux (2012)
- **Psychiatric disorders:** Banner (2013), Howes & Kapur (2009), Disner et al. (2011), Flint & Kendler (2014), Fatemi & Folsom (2009)
- **Sensation / perception / attention:** relevant module texts plus independently sourced empirical literature

These are anchors, not a closed bibliography.

**Band guidance:**

- **60+**: normally requires substantive engagement with relevant module scholarship alongside independent peer-reviewed evidence;
- **70+**: normally requires critical integration of module and independently sourced research;
- **80–85**: requires unusually strong synthesis across theory, methods, levels of analysis and evidence.

If there is no meaningful engagement with identifiable module scholarship, normally:

- C4.1 cannot score 1;
- relevant Knowledge/Application subcriteria should be constrained where concepts are generic;
- Reading and Referencing should not normally enter the First-class band.

Do not enforce a mechanical citation quota beyond the official minimum of 10 scholarly references.

## 6. Narrative-review methodology rule

The assignment explicitly requires a structured narrative review rather than an ordinary essay.

The Methodology section should report, as appropriate:

- databases searched;
- search terms / keyword combinations;
- use of Boolean operators where relevant;
- inclusion/exclusion or scope decisions;
- date, population or methodological restrictions where used;
- limitations of the search strategy;
- reflexivity concerning study selection where appropriate.

### Quality judgement

Strong methodology is not defined by length.

It is defined by whether a reader can understand:

1. **how the literature was found**;
2. **why particular literature was included**;
3. **what boundaries shape the conclusions**.

The methodology does not need to meet systematic-review standards unless the student claims that it does.

C4.3 primarily captures this requirement, with C5.1 assessing structural integration.

---

## 7. Thematic-synthesis rule

The review should not become a serial list of article summaries.

Strong synthesis normally:

- organises evidence around themes, mechanisms, theories or debates;
- compares studies directly;
- explains why results agree or conflict;
- connects differences to methods, samples, measures or levels of analysis;
- develops a justified view of the state of knowledge.

Study-by-study description without synthesis should constrain C3.3 and C5.2.

---

## 8. Scientific-inference and reductionism rule

Biological evidence must be interpreted at the level justified by the method.

Marking should reward distinctions such as:

- neural **correlate** versus neural **cause**;
- local activation versus distributed network;
- necessary versus sufficient mechanism;
- genetic association versus genetic determination;
- neurotransmitter involvement versus complete disorder explanation;
- animal mechanism versus direct human generalisation.

Students should also be able to recognise when molecular, cellular, neural-system and behavioural explanations are complementary rather than competing.

This rule primarily informs C1.3–C1.4, C2.2–C2.3 and C3.1–C3.4.

---

## 9. Required assessment coverage

The review should materially include:

1. **Introduction**
   - background/key concepts;
   - research question or central theme;
   - aims/rationale.

2. **Methodology**
   - search strategy;
   - databases;
   - terms;
   - inclusion/exclusion/scope decisions;
   - relevant limitations/reflexivity.

3. **Findings / thematic synthesis**
   - organised around meaningful themes;
   - theory and empirical evidence;
   - methodological evaluation.

4. **Discussion and conclusion**
   - synthesis of key findings;
   - theoretical/practical implications where appropriate;
   - limitations;
   - gaps/future directions;
   - contribution of biological approaches.

Operational rule:

- one materially underdeveloped component → reflect in relevant subcriteria;
- one essentially absent component → hold for tutor review before borderline uplift;
- two or more essentially absent components → substantive fail candidate.

---

## 10. Reference minimum

The brief requires **at least 10 scholarly references**.

Therefore:

- fewer than 10 scholarly references is a formal non-compliance flag;
- do not invent an automatic numerical deduction unless University policy specifies one;
- reflect consequences within C4.2 and any other genuinely affected criteria;
- hold unusually severe cases for tutor review.

Quality matters as well as number.

Ten weak or irrelevant references do not satisfy high-band reading expectations.

## 12. Cohort calibration targets

### 12.1 Target mean

```text
target_mean = 65
```

Applies after academic eligibility checks and before administrative penalties.

### 12.2 Desired spread

```text
soft_target_population_sd = 8
typical_overall_range ≈ 45–85
```

This is not a quota or clipping rule.

- do not force normality;
- do not create failures to improve distribution;
- do not manufacture 80+ grades;
- genuinely weak work may fall below 45.

### 12.3 Moderation bounds

- preserve meaningful ties;
- preserve evidence-based rank order;
- use common constrained affine moderation;
- criterion movement normally limited to ±5 points.

## 13. Borderline-fail policy

Pass threshold: 40.

```text
if processed_overall in [38, 39]
and all four review components are materially represented
and methodology is present
and at least 10 scholarly references are accounted for
and no essential criterion is wholly absent
and no unresolved evidence remains:
    academic_overall = 40
    record borderline_uplift = true
else:
    retain result or hold for review
```

Do not uplift to satisfy the target mean.

### 13.1 Extreme / substantive failures

Any processed overall below 38 is not automatically finalised.

Required review:

1. verify the full submission;
2. re-check all 20 subcriteria;
3. verify reference count and source accessibility;
4. verify methodology/search section;
5. verify no extraction issue hid evidence;
6. record reason for fail;
7. flag for tutor approval.

---

## 14. Exceptional grades

- routine upper bound: **79**
- exceptional range: **80–85**
- absolute maximum: **85**

Any 80–85 result requires a written rationale.

Exceptional work would normally demonstrate:

- sophisticated topic knowledge;
- critical integration across biological levels of analysis;
- unusually strong methodological reasoning;
- thematic synthesis rather than summary;
- independent and current scholarship;
- explicit handling of contradictory evidence and uncertainty.

---

## 15. Moderation procedure

1. Score all 20 subcriteria using 0/0.5/1 without reference to cohort targets.
2. Assign 20 independent quality marks.
3. Aggregate to five independent criterion marks and weighted overall.
4. Calibrate cross-cohort by comparing the same subcriteria across students.
5. Review:
   - all sub-40 subcriteria;
   - all criterion marks below 40;
   - fail candidates;
   - 80+ candidates;
   - fewer than 10 scholarly references;
   - absent methodology;
   - absent review sections;
   - weak/inappropriate sources;
   - competence/quality inconsistencies.
6. Freeze academic evidence.
7. Apply constrained moderation towards mean 65 and SD 8.
8. Apply eligible 38–39 → 40 rule.
9. Hold remaining fails for tutor review.
10. Jointly round criterion marks to reproduce final academic overall.
11. Report achieved cohort statistics and activated flags.

## 16. Word-count policy

Official limit:

```text
2000 words excluding references
```

The brief states that exceeding the limit by **more than 10%** causes a **10 percentage-point deduction**.

Therefore:

```text
threshold = 2200
penalty = 10 if verified_assessed_word_count > 2200 else 0
final_grade = max(0, academic_grade - penalty)
```

Exactly 2,200 words does not trigger a penalty under “more than 10%”.

No automatic deduction applies for an under-length review.

Use the authorised institutional word-count inclusion/exclusion rules. If a count is ambiguous near 2,200, flag rather than inventing a rule.

Do not renormalise after penalties.

A penalty-induced fail is not automatically uplifted to 40.

## 17. AI and academic-integrity handling

The assessment brief explicitly permits AI as an aid but not as a replacement for student critical thinking and analysis.

When marking:

- do not infer misconduct from writing style alone;
- do not use “sounds AI-generated” as a grading criterion;
- assess the submitted work against the rubric;
- verify factual and citation integrity where concerns arise;
- flag suspected policy breaches through the appropriate academic-integrity process rather than inventing a mark deduction.

Weak engagement with module readings, fabricated citations, unsupported claims or generic synthesis should be marked academically through the relevant criteria regardless of cause.

## 18. Feedback format

For each student:

- exactly 3 strengths;
- exactly 3 priority improvements;
- one paragraph for each of the 5 official criteria;
- official criterion percentages only;
- academic overall and final overall where a penalty changes it;
- two-sentence personalised closing observation.


### Feedback voice and seminar-linking

Student-facing feedback should sound like feedback from the tutor who has taught the module, not a detached rubric summary.

Where it is genuinely relevant to the student's work, make **specific links back to the taught seminars**, for example:

- “As we covered in Seminar 1, …”
- “This connects well with the point we discussed in Seminar 2 about …”
- “For the next assignment, return to the approach we practised in Seminar 3 …”

Rules:

- use the **actual seminar number and topic** supported by the module's seminar materials / `Seminar_Led_Reading_Integration.md`;
- make the seminar reference analytically useful — it should remind the student of a concept, method, reading or activity that helps explain the feedback;
- do not force a seminar reference into every criterion paragraph;
- normally include **at least one useful seminar connection in the overall feedback when an appropriate connection exists**;
- never claim something was taught in a seminar if the source materials do not support that claim.

Use brief, natural encouragement where warranted. Appropriate phrases include:

- “Great job here.”
- “Excellent work.”
- “Nicely done.”
- “This is a real strength.”
- “Good work on this.”
- “You handled this well.”
- “This is developing well.”

Praise must be **evidence-calibrated**:

- reserve “Excellent work” and similarly strong praise for genuinely excellent / First-class evidence;
- use warmer but more measured phrases such as “Nicely done” or “Good work on this” for solid achievement;
- avoid generic praise that is not tied to something the student actually did;
- do not make every paragraph begin or end with praise.

The overall tone should be **warm, encouraging, specific and academically direct**. Areas for improvement should be framed constructively and, where possible, tell the student what to do next rather than merely naming a weakness.

Do not expose:

- diagnostic codes;
- subcriterion quality marks;
- cohort moderation;
- internal academic-integrity flags;
- tutor-review flags.

Use British English.

## 21. Release gates

No grade is released until:

- complete review is accounted for and readable;
- all 20 subcriteria are scored or unresolved;
- methodology/search section has been checked;
- scholarly-reference minimum has been checked;
- every sub-40 subcriterion has been reviewed;
- every fail candidate has been checked;
- every extreme fail has tutor approval;
- every 80–85 result has an exceptional rationale;
- word-count penalty has been independently verified;
- criterion marks reproduce the released overall;
- cohort statistics are reported honestly;
- feedback matches the final grade-sheet run.
