# Biological Psychology Narrative Literature Review — Criteria and Diagnostic Subcriteria

## Assessment

**Unit 9 Narrative Literature Review**

- **Word count:** 2,000 words excluding references
- **Weighting:** 70%
- **Minimum academic references:** 10
- Students choose one topic:
  - Biology of Sensation, Perception and Attention
  - Biology of Consciousness
  - Biology of Learning and Memory
  - Biology of Language
  - Biology of Emotions
  - Biology of Psychiatric Disorders

The review should include an introduction, transparent methodology/search section, thematic synthesis, and discussion/conclusion.

## Official criteria and weightings

1. **Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues — 25%**
2. **Application of Theory to Practice and/or Real-world Example — 25%**
3. **Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%**
4. **Reading and Referencing — 15%**
5. **Presentation Style and Structure — 10%**

The official criteria and weightings remain unchanged. The four subcriteria below operationalise them for this assessment.

---

# 1. Knowledge and Understanding — 25%

## 1.1 Knowledge of the selected biological-psychology topic
The student demonstrates accurate, appropriately detailed knowledge of the central biological mechanisms, neural structures/systems and psychological processes relevant to the chosen topic.

## 1.2 Understanding of major theories and conceptual debates
The review accurately explains the principal theoretical frameworks, competing explanations or conceptual controversies relevant to the topic rather than presenting only isolated findings.

## 1.3 Understanding of biological levels of analysis
The student demonstrates understanding of how evidence at relevant molecular, cellular, systems/network and behavioural/cognitive levels contributes to the topic, without assuming that one level automatically provides a complete explanation.

## 1.4 Understanding of methods and what they can establish
The student demonstrates accurate knowledge of the key methods represented in the literature and recognises the different kinds of inference supported by imaging, lesions, EEG/ERP, stimulation, animal work, genetics or other methods relevant to the topic.

---

# 2. Application of Theory to Practice / Real-world Example — 25%

## 2.1 Application of biological theory to empirical evidence
The student uses theoretical concepts or biological mechanisms to interpret empirical findings rather than presenting theory and evidence in separate descriptive sections.

## 2.2 Application across levels of analysis
The student connects biological mechanisms to cognition, emotion or behaviour and, where appropriate, links cellular/system-level processes to observable psychological outcomes.

## 2.3 Application of methodological knowledge to substantive claims
The student uses knowledge of research methods to judge what empirical findings mean for the chosen theory or biological explanation, rather than treating all evidence as equivalent.

## 2.4 Application to real-world, clinical or functional implications where appropriate
The student explains the relevance of the reviewed evidence to real-world function, clinical understanding, rehabilitation, diagnosis, intervention or broader psychological explanation where this is appropriate to the chosen topic. This should not be forced where the topic is primarily theoretical.

---

# 3. Critical Analysis and Evaluation — 25%

## 3.1 Critical evaluation of theories and biological explanations
The student evaluates strengths, limitations, assumptions and explanatory scope of competing theories rather than accepting biological accounts at face value.

## 3.2 Critical evaluation of empirical methods and evidence
The student evaluates design quality, measurement, sample limitations, confounding, generalisability and inferential strength of key studies, with attention to what different biological methods can and cannot show.

## 3.3 Comparative thematic synthesis
Evidence is compared and synthesised across studies and methods to develop themes, agreements, contradictions or patterns rather than being summarised study-by-study.

## 3.4 Judgement, uncertainty and research gaps
The student develops justified conclusions about the current state of knowledge, recognises uncertainty and limitations, and identifies meaningful gaps or priorities for future research.

---

# 4. Reading and Referencing — 15%

## 4.1 Substantive engagement with module/seminar scholarship
The review meaningfully uses relevant module readings and seminar-linked sources rather than relying entirely on generic external material. For review methodology, Snyder (2019) and/or Sukhera (2022) provide key anchors. Topic-specific module readings should also be used where relevant.

## 4.2 Breadth, quality and independence of the literature base
The review includes at least 10 academic references and demonstrates appropriate use of high-quality peer-reviewed primary studies and reviews, including independently sourced literature beyond the module list.

## 4.3 Transparent and appropriate literature-search process
The methodology section clearly reports databases, search terms and inclusion/exclusion or scope decisions, with sufficient transparency to understand how the literature base was constructed. Limitations and reflexivity are acknowledged where appropriate.

## 4.4 Evidence-to-claim alignment and reference accuracy
Sources substantively support the claims made, and in-text citations/reference-list entries are accurate and consistent with APA or another authorised programme style.

---

# 5. Presentation Style and Structure — 10%

## 5.1 Appropriate narrative-review structure
The review contains a clear introduction, search/methodology section, thematic findings/synthesis, and discussion/conclusion, with appropriate balance across sections.

## 5.2 Thematic rather than study-by-study organisation
Headings and paragraphs are organised around concepts, mechanisms, debates or themes, enabling comparison and synthesis rather than a sequence of article summaries.

## 5.3 Coherence, academic style and conceptual precision
The argument progresses logically, terminology is accurate, writing is concise and appropriately formal, and distinctions such as correlate versus cause or localisation versus network explanation are communicated precisely.

## 5.4 Technical presentation and assessment requirements
The work adheres to the 2,000-word requirement, reference expectations and formatting guidance, with accurate grammar, spelling, punctuation and professional presentation.

---

# Reading-engagement anchors

The assessment should reward **use of scholarship**, not a citation checklist.

## Review-method anchors

Strong reviews should normally show that the student understands narrative review as a method, using:

- **Snyder (2019)** and/or
- **Sukhera (2022)**

to inform the review's search, selection, synthesis and limitations.

## Topic-specific anchors

Students should then engage with relevant module readings for their chosen topic. Examples include:

- **Consciousness:** Rees et al. (2002), Gazzaniga (2005), Zeman (2001)
- **Learning and memory:** Eichenbaum (2011) plus relevant memory/amnesia evidence
- **Language:** Price (2012), Friederici (2011)
- **Emotion:** LeDoux (2012)
- **Psychiatric disorders:** Banner (2013), Howes & Kapur (2009), Disner et al. (2011), Flint & Kendler (2014), Fatemi & Folsom (2009)
- **Sensation/perception/attention:** relevant Ward/Banich readings plus independently sourced empirical literature

These are anchors, not a closed bibliography.

## Band expectation

For a **2:1 or above**, there should normally be substantive engagement with relevant module scholarship alongside independent peer-reviewed evidence.

For a **First**, module and independent sources should be critically integrated across theory, methods and findings.

A source does not count as substantive engagement if it is merely listed, cited for a generic statement, or reproduced without evaluation.

---

# Diagnostic use

Rate each subcriterion:

- **1** = fully and convincingly demonstrated
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously demonstrated
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

These ratings support, but do not mechanically determine, the official criterion percentage.
