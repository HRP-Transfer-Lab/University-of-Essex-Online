## Aims

This seminar focuses on the phenomenon of **change blindness**, explored through the UoEO Psychology Lab change-blindness task.

### This week we will

- Discuss and reflect on experience with the change-blindness task.
- Learn about explanations for change blindness from cognitive psychology research.
- Consider real-world impacts of limited attention and change blindness.
- Collaboratively evaluate peer explanations using evidence from:
  - cognitive psychology;
  - cognitive neuroscience;
  - cognitive neuropsychology.
- Reflect on the use of “cognitive tasks” in cognitive psychology.

## Test Your Knowledge

- The change-blindness phenomenon demonstrated in the task.
- Explanations based on concepts such as attention and awareness.
- Real-world situations affected by change blindness.
- Relevant evidence from cognitive psychology, cognitive neuroscience and cognitive neuropsychology.

## Social Learning

Provide constructive feedback to peers based on relevant theories and evidence.

## Check Your Understanding

1. What surprised you about your experience with the Change Blindness Task?
2. How might explanations from cognitive neuroscience or cognitive neuropsychology complement or challenge explanations from cognitive psychology?

## Discuss & Debate

1. Is change blindness solely a failure of attention, or might other cognitive factors be involved?
2. What are some important real-world contexts where recognising changes is critical for safety or performance? When might change blindness occur in these settings?
