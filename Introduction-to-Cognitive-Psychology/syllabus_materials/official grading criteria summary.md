# Official Grading Criteria Summary — Introduction to Cognitive Psychology

This summary records the exact official **NQF Level 4** criterion weights from the uploaded grading rubrics. It is intended to prevent accidental carry-over of criterion structures from other modules.

---

# Assessment 1 — CLQ / Discussion Forum

## Official weights

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues | **25%** |
| Evaluation and Analytical Skills | **25%** |
| Communication | **25%** |
| Reading & Referencing | **25%** |

## Assessment-specific rubric cues

### Knowledge and Understanding — 25%
Reflection should focus on:

- personal participation in the online Change Blindness task;
- core knowledge;
- real-world application.

### Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 25%
Analysis should particularly use approaches from:

1. Cognitive Psychology;
2. Cognitive Neuroscience;
3. Cognitive Neuropsychology.

### Communication — 25%
The rubric considers:

- timing of initial and peer posts;
- relevance to audience/situation/complexity;
- reflective and insightful communication;
- coherence and contribution to peers;
- communicative style, structure and clarity.

### Reading & Referencing — 25%
The rubric expects:

- course textbooks and journal articles;
- limited reliance on websites;
- developing wider reading beyond platform materials;
- accurate referencing.

# Assessment 2 — Scientific Report

## Official weights

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues | **20%** |
| Application of Theory to Practice and/or Real-world Example | **20%** |
| Evaluation and Analytical Skills | **20%** |
| Reading and Referencing | **20%** |
| Presentation Style and Structure | **20%** |

## Assessment-specific rubric cues

### Knowledge and Understanding — 20%
The rubric explicitly expects a grasp of:

- the experiment's purpose;
- relevant cognitive theories;
- how the experiment/theory connects to broader contexts.

### Application of Theory — 20%
Where applicable, students should apply theory to practice and/or real-world examples and demonstrate links between theory and the research context.

### Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 20%
The rubric explicitly expects students to:

- formulate a testable hypothesis;
- critique the study design, including variables and procedure;
- evaluate alignment with the research question;
- analyse data;
- draw evidence-based conclusions;
- discuss findings in relation to theoretical frameworks.

### Reading and Referencing — 20%
The rubric expects:

- course textbooks and journal articles;
- limited use of websites for information;
- developing wider reading;
- meaningful use of sources to inform the experiment's design, hypothesis and discussion;
- accurate referencing.

### Presentation Style and Structure — 20%
The rubric evaluates:

- clarity;
- coherent structure;
- scientific/academic writing style;
- grammar, punctuation and spelling;
- adherence to scientific writing standards.

# Grade-band principle

Both rubrics use the official Level 4 bands:

- **80–100:** Exceptional / First
- **70–79:** First
- **60–69:** 2:1
- **50–59:** 2:2
- **40–49:** Third / Pass
- **30–39:** Marginal Fail
- **20–30:** Fail
- **Below 20:** Fail

The official rubrics remain authoritative. Any later subcriteria or grading specifications must sit underneath these exact criteria and weights.
