# Introduction to Cognitive Psychology — Syllabus and Assessments

## Module Home

#### Hi Mark! Welcome to the Introduction to Cognitive Psychology Module.

![Module home image](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1651818/course/section/293821/ICP%20Module%20Home%20Image.jpg)

This module aims to provide you with a wide-ranging understanding of how the human mind works, from basic processes like perception and memory to complex functions like decision-making, emotion regulation and creativity. We will look at these functions through the lens of three approaches within the field: cognitive psychology, cognitive neuroscience, and cognitive neuropsychology.

During this module, you'll participate in hands-on cognitive tasks and experiments via the Psychology Technical Hub. This experience should deepen your understanding of research within the field of cognitive psychology and familiarise you with methods that will be helpful for your own research later in your undergraduate programme in psychology.

Get ready to embark on a journey into psychology from the ‘information processing’ perspective that will deepen your understanding of the human mind and its applications in the real world.

## Module Structure

- **Unit 1:** Approaches to cognition and ethics — cognitive psychology, cognitive neuroscience, cognitive neuropsychology, and ethical guidelines.
- **Unit 2:** Visual perception and attention — Two Visual Systems theory, selective attention and change blindness, attention and consciousness.
- **Unit 3:** Top-down vs bottom-up processing — top-down and bottom-up attention, executive functioning and working memory, automatic processing.
- **Unit 4:** Long-term memory and knowledge structures — declarative memory, semantic networks and knowledge structures, non-declarative memory.
- **Unit 5:** Learning, reconstructive memory and forgetting — memory encoding and learning methods, reconstructive memory and false memories, effects of emotion on learning.
- **Unit 6:** Emotion and cognition — emotion and decision-making, emotional intelligence, emotion regulation.
- **Unit 7:** Language comprehension and production — reading comprehension, speech production, discourse processing.
- **Unit 8:** Hypothesis testing, problem solving and decision-making — hypothesis testing, types of reasoning, problem solving and creative insight, decision-making.
- **Unit 9:** Applications in translational cognitive psychology — applied decision-making, cognitive bias modification, brain plasticity.

### Psychology Technical Hub

The [**Psychology Technical Hub**](https://www.my-course.co.uk/course/view.php?id=7821&section=0) supports research methods and technical skills, including self-directed learning materials, software demonstrations, workshops, study resources and access to Experimental Officer support.

## Module Aims and Learning Outcomes

### Module aims

- Explore the main areas of cognitive psychology and key approaches and methods used in modern cognitive psychology research.
- Carry out a systematic literature search using academic databases to review a topic, and evaluate and demonstrate an understanding of theories, hypotheses, evidence, and interpretations.
- Exercise critical thinking and creativity in applying cognitive psychology to real-world situations.
- Develop skills in critical thinking, evidence evaluation, communication and ethical practice in the field.
- Participate in online cognitive tasks and experiments.
- Write a research report of an experiment in cognitive psychology using APA formatting.

### On successful completion of this module, you should be able to:

1. Demonstrate basic knowledge of topics in cognitive psychology and key approaches and methods used in modern cognitive psychology research.
2. Carry out a literature search using an academic database to review a topic, and evaluate and demonstrate an understanding of theories, hypotheses, evidence, and interpretations.
3. Demonstrate critical understanding of an application of cognitive psychology to real-world situations.
4. Demonstrate ability in critical thinking, evidence evaluation, clear and precise communication, and knowledge of and respect for ethical standards.
5. Write a research report, or parts of a report, using APA format.

# Assessment 1 — Units 3–4 Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ)

## Purpose

The collaborative learning activity promotes higher-level thinking, communication skills, self-management and leadership through student interaction, engagement with course materials and wider reading.

Students submit one initial post and two peer responses. All three assessed posts are judged against the official grading criteria.

## Question

### Initial post — Unit 3, 500 words

After completing the **Change Blindness Experiment**, with optional help from the associated Reflective Exercise in Unit 2:

1. Describe, with supporting research, the change blindness phenomenon as demonstrated by the task you participated in. Then provide an evidence-based explanation for this phenomenon, drawing upon module readings and materials from Units 1, 2, 3 and 4 where relevant.
2. Suggest one or more real-world situations where the limitations of attention implied by this phenomenon could have a significant impact on day-to-day functioning.

### Follow-up responses — Unit 4, two posts of 300 words each

Critically respond to at least two peers' posts by evaluating their explanations of change blindness, guided by research from **two** of the following approaches:

1. Cognitive Psychology.
2. Cognitive Neuroscience.
3. Cognitive Neuropsychology.

In this evaluation you can either challenge or elaborate on the explanation, based on evidence, concepts or theories from two of these approaches.

Draw from relevant resources and readings from Units 1–4.

## Instructions

- Submit one **500-word Initial Response**.
- Submit two **300-word Peer Responses**.
- Use an academic tone and evidence-based argument supported by psychological literature.
- The CLQ runs for **two weeks**, from the start of Unit 3 to the end of Unit 4.
- The initial response must be posted in the first week; both peer responses by the end of the second week.
- **All three assessed responses must be submitted to pass.**
- Label posts Initial Response, Peer Response 1 and Peer Response 2.
- Follow-up posts must be clearly connected to previous posts and show reflection on peers' work.
- Students are encouraged to make informal contributions throughout the activity to support learning and demonstrate animation/leadership.
- Up to **five informal posts** can be made; their content is not assessed and does not count towards the word count.
- Posts must be entered directly into the discussion forum text box rather than attached as Word/PDF files.
- The assessment cannot be blind marked because tutors actively participate in the discussions.
- Students on combined programmes should identify their authorised referencing style in the reference-list heading where this differs from APA.
- [**CLQ grading tips**](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1651878/mod_forum/intro/CLQ%20Grading%20Criteria%20Tips.pdf)

### Word-count rule

If the assessment exceeds the applicable word-count limit or range by **more than 10%**, the awarded grade is reduced by **10 percentage points**.

No grade reduction applies for being under the word limit/range.

## Learning Outcomes listed for this assessment

- Critically evaluate the evidence that thinking can be improved.
- Demonstrate application of the relationship between acquisition, retention and retrieval of knowledge to real-world situations.

> **Source note:** These are reproduced as supplied in the official assessment material, even though their wording appears more closely related to other cognitive topics than to the change-blindness task.

# Formative Assessment — Unit 7 Draft Literature Review

This activity is **optional and non-assessed**, due at the end of Unit 7. Approximate word count: **850 words**.

Its purpose is to prepare students for the Unit 9 Scientific Report.

Students conduct a draft literature review relating to either:

- the **Misinformation Effect Experiment** completed in Unit 5; or
- the **Emotional Stroop Effect Experiment** completed in Unit 6.

## Required formative components

- Reduced PRISMA-P Rationale and Method Form.
- PRISMA-P flow diagram.
- Draft Literature Review.
- References section with a maximum of 10 references.

Resources:

- [Reduced PRISMA-P Rationale and Method Form](https://www.my-course.co.uk/pluginfile.php/1651903/mod_assign/intro/REDUCED%20PRISMA-P%20-%20Rationale%20and%20Method.docx?time=1711539225268)
- [PRISMA website](http://www.prisma-statement.org/)

## Draft Literature Review Structure

### Introduction
- Briefly introduce the research question.
- Summarise the PRISMA-P-based method being used.

### Previous Research
- Summarise findings from original/seminal studies and their theoretical interpretation.
- Summarise more recent studies and their theoretical interpretation.

### Real-World Application
- Discuss relevance to real-world situations.

### Conclusion
- Summarise the main points.

# Assessment 2 — Unit 9 Scientific Report

**Word count:** 2,500 words  
**Due:** End of Unit 9

Students write a Scientific Report based on one of the two experiments completed during the module:

- **Misinformation Effect Experiment** — Unit 5; or
- **Emotional Stroop Experiment** — Unit 6.

The report uses class results supplied by the tutor and should incorporate the Unit 7 Formative Draft Literature Review. The same sources from that literature review should be used in the Discussion section.

## Required Structure and Suggested Word Counts

- **Title Page**
- **Abstract — 250 words**
- **Literature Review — 850 words**
  - Background
  - Prior Findings
  - Theory
  - Rationale and Hypothesis
- **Method — 650 words**
  - Participants
  - Design
  - Materials
  - Procedure
  - Ethics
- **Results** — provided by tutor
- **Discussion — 750 words**
  - Overview
  - Comparison with Research
  - Theoretical Interpretations
  - Research Evaluation
  - Implications and Future Directions
  - Conclusion
- **References**

Students should use the official **Quantitative Report Template**.
