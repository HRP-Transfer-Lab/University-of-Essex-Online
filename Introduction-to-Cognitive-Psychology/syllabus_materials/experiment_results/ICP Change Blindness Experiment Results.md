## Paradigm

### Experimental condition

A **full scene** is presented, followed by a blank/grey interval and the changed full scene, repeated across **5 trials**.

Approximate timing shown in the source:
- image: ~1 sec
- interruption: ~1 sec
- image: ~1 sec

### Control condition

An **isolated scene element** is presented, followed by a blank/grey interval and the changed isolated element, repeated across **5 trials**.

Approximate timing shown in the source:
- image: ~1 sec
- interruption: ~1 sec
- image: ~1 sec

The **full scene (experimental condition)** versus **isolated scene element (control condition)** are the two values of the independent variable (IV).

The dependent variable (DV) is **reaction time**.

## Reaction Times for Each Condition

Based on **70 participants** taking the ICP modules. The averages are reported after removal of outliers/extreme reaction times.

| Condition | Average reaction time |
|---|---:|
| Control condition | **3.65 seconds** |
| Experimental condition | **15.68 seconds** |

## Is There a Significant Difference Between Experimental and Control Conditions?

**Yes. The difference is highly significant (p < 0.001).**

The reaction time for the Experimental Condition (full scene) is on average around **12 seconds longer** than for the Control Condition (isolated element).

## Notes from the Source

1. For students who have had Research Methods, outliers were removed using the **interquartile range (IQR) method**, identifying points beyond **1.5 times the IQR from the quartiles**.
2. Statistical significance was determined using a **mixed-effects model**. The source explicitly notes that students do not need to know this analysis.
