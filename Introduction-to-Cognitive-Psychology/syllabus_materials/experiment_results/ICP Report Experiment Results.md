# Misinformation Effect Experiment

## Sample and design

The total number of participants was **99 from 6 ICP modules**.

Participants were randomly assigned to the **“smashed”** or **“contacted”** verb conditions. One group was exposed to the “smashed” condition and the other to the “contacted” condition.

The source text reports 54 participants in one condition and 45 in the other; the descriptive-statistics table identifies **Smashed N = 45** and **Contacted N = 54**.

## Table 1. Descriptive statistics

| Measure | Smashed | Contacted | Significant? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Speed in mph (avg) | 36 | 35 | NS |
| Number of cars (avg) | 5 | 5 | NS |
| Percent correct car colours | 82 | 81 | NS |
| Percent “smashed” glass | 50 | 51 | NS |
| N (number of participants) | 45 | 54 | — |

**NS:** Not a significant difference.

## Statistical interpretation supplied in the source

- There was **no significant difference** between average estimates of the speed of impact in the “smashed” versus “contacted” conditions.
- There was **no significant difference** between recollections of whether or not any glass smashed in the “smashed” versus “contacted” conditions.
- Recollections of the **number of cars** and **car colours** were the same in both conditions, which the source interprets as indicating the same level of attentiveness to the task in both groups.

# Emotional Stroop Experiment

## Sample and design

The total number of participants was **75 from 6 ICP modules**.

Each participant was exposed to all three word-valence conditions:

- neutral;
- positive;
- negative.

## Table 1. Descriptive statistics

| Reaction time | Neutral | Positive | Negative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Average/Mean (msec) | **485** | **450** | **472** |
| Minimum (msec) | 280 | 230 | 212 |
| Maximum (msec) | 984 | 960 | 1013 |

## Statistical interpretation supplied in the source

There was a **significant difference** between average reaction times for deciding whether the colour was red or blue across the three word-valence conditions.

The source reports:

**p = 0.002**

and notes that this is below the p = 0.05 threshold used to decide whether a difference is statistically significant. It also explicitly notes that this information is **not required in the report**, although tutors may discuss the concept.

### Pairwise comparisons

- **Neutral vs Positive:** significant difference.
- **Negative vs Positive:** significant difference.
- **Neutral vs Negative:** no significant difference.

## Table 2. Comparisons between conditions

| Comparison | p | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Neutral – Positive | **0.002** | Significant difference |
| Negative – Positive | **0.003** | Significant difference |
| Neutral – Negative | Not reported numerically | No significant difference |
