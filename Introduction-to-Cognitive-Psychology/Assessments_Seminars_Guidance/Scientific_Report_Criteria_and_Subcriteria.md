# Introduction to Cognitive Psychology Scientific Report — Criteria and Diagnostic Subcriteria

## Assessment

**Unit 9 Scientific Report — 2,500 words**

Students write a Scientific Report based on one of the module experiments:

- **Misinformation Effect Experiment**; or
- **Emotional Stroop Experiment**.

The report should integrate the Unit 7 formative literature-review work and use the class results provided by the tutor.

Required structure:

- Title Page
- Abstract
- Literature Review
- Method
- Results
- Discussion
- References

## Official criteria and weightings

1. **Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues — 20%**
2. **Application of Theory to Practice and/or Real-world Example — 20%**
3. **Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 20%**
4. **Reading and Referencing — 20%**
5. **Presentation Style and Structure — 20%**

The official rubric remains authoritative. The four subcriteria beneath each criterion are diagnostic prompts.

---

# C1. Knowledge and Understanding — 20%

## C1.1 Understanding of the experiment's purpose and cognitive phenomenon
The student accurately explains the research problem, experimental purpose and relevant cognitive phenomenon for the chosen Misinformation Effect or Emotional Stroop experiment.

## C1.2 Understanding of relevant cognitive theories
The student accurately explains the principal cognitive theory or theories needed to interpret the experiment and distinguishes theoretical explanation from a simple description of findings.

## C1.3 Understanding of design, variables and procedure
The student demonstrates accurate understanding of the study design, variables, task/procedure and the logic linking these to the research question and hypothesis.

## C1.4 Understanding of broader context, ethics and implications
The student places the experiment within an appropriate broader cognitive, ethical and/or applied context, including relevant real-world implications without overgeneralising beyond the evidence.

---

# C2. Application of Theory to Practice / Real-world Example — 20%

## C2.1 Theory is applied to the rationale and hypothesis
The Literature Review uses theory and prior findings to justify the study rationale and generate a clear, testable hypothesis rather than merely describing background literature.

## C2.2 Theory is applied to interpretation of the class results
The Discussion uses relevant theory to explain the observed pattern of results, including null or unexpected findings where applicable.

## C2.3 Research evidence is applied to the specific experiment
Prior studies are used to contextualise the current design and findings, with clear links between published evidence and the specific class experiment.

## C2.4 Real-world application is justified and proportionate
The report develops a plausible real-world application or implication of the findings and explains the link rather than making broad claims unsupported by the study.

---

# C3. Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 20%

## C3.1 Testable hypothesis and alignment with the research question
The report states a precise testable hypothesis and demonstrates alignment among the research question, variables, design and predicted outcome.

## C3.2 Accurate and evidence-based interpretation of results
The student accurately reports and interprets the supplied class results, distinguishes statistical outcome from theoretical interpretation, and does not claim effects that the data do not show.

## C3.3 Critical evaluation of study design and evidence
The Discussion identifies meaningful strengths and limitations involving design, variables, procedure, sample, measurement, validity, generalisability or other relevant methodological issues and explains their consequences.

## C3.4 Integration, judgement and alternative interpretation
The student compares the findings with prior literature, considers plausible alternative explanations, and develops a coherent evidence-based judgement about what the study contributes.

---

# C4. Reading and Referencing — 20%

## C4.1 Structured literature searching and source selection
The student demonstrates the ability to locate relevant academic literature, drawing on the Unit 7 formative systematic-search work where completed, and selects sources appropriate to the experiment and hypothesis.

## C4.2 Breadth, quality and relevance of research literature
The report uses an appropriate range of scholarly sources, including research-informed literature and wider reading beyond only taught material where relevant.

## C4.3 Meaningful use of sources in design, hypothesis and discussion
Sources are used to inform the experiment's rationale, hypothesis and interpretation rather than appearing as disconnected summaries or decorative citations.

## C4.4 Evidence-to-claim alignment and referencing accuracy
Claims are supported by the cited evidence, sources are represented accurately, and APA or another authorised referencing style is applied consistently.

---

# C5. Presentation Style and Structure — 20%

## C5.1 Complete and appropriate scientific-report structure
The report contains the required sections and each section performs its expected scientific function, with appropriate balance across Abstract, Literature Review, Method, Results and Discussion.

## C5.2 Scientific-writing conventions and section discipline
The writing follows scientific-report conventions: Method is replicable and appropriately objective, Results reports outcomes without discussion, and Discussion interprets rather than repeats the Results.

## C5.3 Clarity, coherence and precision
Ideas and arguments are logically ordered, transitions are clear, terminology is used precisely, and grammar, spelling and punctuation support readability.

## C5.4 APA-style presentation and technical completeness
The report follows the required template and APA conventions where applicable, including headings, citations/references, tables/figures and professional presentation.

# Experiment-specific interpretation notes

## Misinformation Effect option

The supplied class data report **no significant differences** between the “smashed” and “contacted” conditions on the listed outcomes.

Students should therefore be rewarded for:

- accurately treating the class result as a null finding;
- comparing that result with prior misinformation literature;
- exploring plausible methodological or theoretical explanations without pretending the predicted effect occurred;
- avoiding the claim that a non-significant result proves that wording has no effect on memory.

## Emotional Stroop option

The supplied class data report:

- a significant overall difference across valence conditions;
- significant Neutral–Positive and Negative–Positive comparisons;
- no significant Neutral–Negative difference.

Students should therefore be rewarded for:

- accurately representing the actual pattern;
- interpreting the positive condition carefully rather than forcing the results into a generic “negative emotion slows responses” narrative;
- comparing this pattern with relevant Emotional Stroop and emotion-cognition literature.

---

# Diagnostic use

Rate each subcriterion:

- **1** = fully and convincingly demonstrated
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously demonstrated
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

These ratings support, but do not mechanically determine, the official criterion percentage.
