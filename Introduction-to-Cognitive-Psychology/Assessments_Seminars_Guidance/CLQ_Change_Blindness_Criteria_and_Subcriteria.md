# Introduction to Cognitive Psychology CLQ — Criteria and Diagnostic Subcriteria

## Assessment

**Units 3–4 Collaborative Learning Question: Change Blindness**

Students submit:

- one **500-word Initial Response** based on their participation in the Change Blindness task;
- two **300-word Peer Responses**;
- informal discussion contributions may support animation/leadership, but their content is not itself assessed.

The Initial Response requires students to:

1. describe the change-blindness phenomenon demonstrated by the task;
2. explain it using research and relevant Unit 1–4 module material;
3. identify real-world situations where limitations of attention may matter.

The two Peer Responses require students to critically evaluate peers' explanations using research from **two of three approaches**:

- Cognitive Psychology;
- Cognitive Neuroscience;
- Cognitive Neuropsychology.

## Official criteria and weightings

1. **Knowledge and Understanding of the Subject Area / Conceptual Issues — 25%**
2. **Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 25%**
3. **Communication — 25%**
4. **Reading & Referencing — 25%**

The official rubric remains authoritative. The four subcriteria beneath each criterion are diagnostic prompts.

---

# C1. Knowledge and Understanding — 25%

## C1.1 Accurate understanding of the Change Blindness task and phenomenon
The student accurately describes what happened in the task, including the difficulty of detecting changes in the full-scene condition, and demonstrates reflective awareness of their participation rather than giving only a generic definition.

## C1.2 Accurate explanation using attention and awareness
The student explains change blindness using relevant cognitive concepts such as selective attention, limited attentional capacity, visual awareness, comparison across views, top-down/bottom-up processing or related constructs, without confusing description with explanation.

## C1.3 Integration of Unit 1–4 module knowledge
The student meaningfully connects the explanation to relevant module material from Units 1–4, including the different approaches to cognition where appropriate, rather than treating change blindness as an isolated fact.

## C1.4 Understanding of real-world implications
The student identifies one or more plausible real-world situations and accurately explains why change blindness or limited attention could affect safety, performance or everyday functioning in those settings.

---

# C2. Evaluation and Analytical Skills — 25%

## C2.1 Evidence-based evaluation of explanations
The student evaluates the adequacy of explanations for change blindness using relevant empirical evidence rather than simply repeating a theory or agreeing/disagreeing with a peer.

## C2.2 Comparative use of cognitive approaches
Across the peer responses, the student substantively uses research from at least **two** of Cognitive Psychology, Cognitive Neuroscience and Cognitive Neuropsychology to challenge, extend or qualify explanations.

## C2.3 Consideration of alternatives, limits and inferential strength
The student recognises that change blindness need not have a single simple explanation and considers limitations, competing mechanisms, task constraints or what particular evidence can and cannot establish.

## C2.4 Critical engagement with peers' specific arguments
Each Peer Response analyses the claims and evidence in the peer's post, identifies a substantive point to challenge or elaborate, and develops a reasoned judgement rather than offering generic praise or an unrelated mini-essay.

---

# C3. Communication — 25%

## C3.1 Clear and coherent Initial Response
The Initial Response communicates the task experience, explanation and real-world application in a logical, concise structure appropriate to the 500-word format.

## C3.2 Peer responses are connected and reflective
Each Peer Response is clearly connected to the peer's preceding post and demonstrates reflection on that student's explanation and evidence.

## C3.3 Constructive academic dialogue and leadership
Communication is respectful, academically appropriate and designed to move the discussion forward. Where evidenced, the student adds useful questions, clarification, synthesis or direction to peers.

## C3.4 Precision, structure and audience-appropriate expression
Key concepts are explained accurately, claims are appropriately qualified, writing is clear and structured, and the level of detail suits an academic discussion forum rather than an essay pasted into a thread.

---

# C4. Reading & Referencing — 25%

## C4.1 Substantive engagement with core module evidence
The student uses relevant module sources to support reasoning about change blindness. Particularly relevant anchors include Rensink, O'Regan and Clark (1997), Posner (2012), Milner and Goodale, and relevant Unit 3 attention material.

## C4.2 Appropriate breadth and quality of scholarly evidence
The student draws on suitable scholarly sources beyond a single textbook or webpage. Higher-quality work uses journal articles or authoritative academic sources appropriately and shows some wider reading where relevant.

## C4.3 Evidence-to-claim alignment
Citations genuinely support the claims for which they are used, and findings are represented accurately rather than cited tokenistically or overstated.

## C4.4 Accurate and consistent referencing
In-text citations and reference-list entries are complete and consistent with APA or another authorised programme referencing style.

---

# Reading-engagement principle

For a **2:1 or above**, there should normally be identifiable substantive engagement with relevant module scholarship, not only generic external sources.

For a **First**, the student should normally integrate:

- their task experience;
- module theory;
- relevant empirical evidence;
- evidence from at least two cognitive approaches in the peer responses;

into a coherent critical explanation.

A source does **not** count as substantive engagement when it is:

- present only in the reference list;
- attached to a generic sentence without being used;
- quoted or paraphrased without connection to the argument;
- cited as evidence for a stronger claim than the source supports.

---

# Diagnostic use

Rate each subcriterion:

- **1** = fully and convincingly demonstrated
- **0.5** = partially, unevenly or ambiguously demonstrated
- **0** = absent, seriously incorrect or not meaningfully demonstrated

These ratings support, but do not mechanically determine, the official criterion percentage.
