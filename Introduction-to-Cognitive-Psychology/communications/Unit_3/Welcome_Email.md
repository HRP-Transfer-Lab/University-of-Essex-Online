# Unit 3 Welcome Email

**Subject:** Welcome to Unit 3 – Processing, Attention and your CLQ

Hi everyone,

Welcome to **Unit 3 of Introduction to Cognitive Psychology**.

This week, we build on the visual perception and attention material from Unit 2 by looking more closely at **how attention and cognitive control are organised**. In particular, we will consider how our behaviour can be shaped both by information arriving from the environment and by our own goals, expectations and knowledge.

The main topics for Unit 3 are:

- **top-down and bottom-up processing and attention**;
- **executive functioning**;
- **working memory**;
- **automatic processing**.

These ideas are useful in their own right, but they also give you some additional tools for thinking about the **Change Blindness Experiment** you completed in Unit 2. For example, when we fail to notice a change, is this simply because the visual information was not available, or because attention was not directed towards and maintained on the relevant information? How might our goals, expectations, working-memory limitations or executive control influence what we notice?

As you work through the Unit 3 material, a useful question to keep in mind is:

> ***Why can something be present in the visual environment and yet fail to reach conscious awareness?***

## Unit 3 readings

The required readings develop these themes from several angles. In particular:

- **Katsuki and Constantinidis (2014)** examine bottom-up and top-down attention and their overlapping neural systems.
- **Diamond (2013)** provides an overview of executive functions and cognitive control.
- **Logie** introduces working memory and the systems that allow us to maintain and manipulate information over short periods.
- **Li et al. (2021)** consider executive functions as predictors of critical thinking.
- **Eysenck and Keane (2020), Chapters 5 and 6**, provide the broader textbook background.

You do not need to force every Unit 3 concept into your assessment. Instead, use the ideas and evidence that genuinely help you explain the phenomenon you observed.

## Your first assessment begins this week: the CLQ

The **Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ)** runs across **Units 3 and 4** and is based on the Change Blindness Experiment.

There are **three assessed components**:

1. **Initial Response — 500 words, posted during Unit 3**
2. **Peer Response 1 — 300 words, posted during Unit 4**
3. **Peer Response 2 — 300 words, posted during Unit 4**

**All three assessed responses must be submitted to pass the assessment.**

For your **500-word Initial Response**, you are asked to:

- describe the change-blindness phenomenon demonstrated in the task, supported by research;
- provide an **evidence-based explanation** for why change blindness occurs, drawing on relevant module material and readings from Units 1–4;
- identify one or more **real-world situations** in which limitations of attention of this kind could have an important impact on everyday functioning.

A strong response will move beyond simply saying what happened in the task. Try to separate:

> ***what the task demonstrated → why it may have happened → what we can reasonably infer from it.***

Your notes from the Unit 2 Change Blindness Experiment should therefore be useful. If you have not yet completed the task, please do so before developing your CLQ response.

In **Unit 4**, you will then respond critically to two classmates. Across those peer responses, you will evaluate or extend their explanations using research from at least **two** of the following approaches:

- cognitive psychology;
- cognitive neuroscience;
- cognitive neuropsychology.

The CLQ is collaborative, so your responses should engage with the particular argument your classmate has made rather than operating as two separate mini-essays.

Please also remember to label the assessed posts clearly as **Initial Response**, **Peer Response 1** and **Peer Response 2**, and enter them directly into the discussion forum rather than uploading them as attachments.
