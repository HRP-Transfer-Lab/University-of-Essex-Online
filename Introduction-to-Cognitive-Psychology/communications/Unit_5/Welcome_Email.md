# Unit 5 Welcome Email

**Subject:** Welcome to Unit 5 – Learning, Memory, Reconstruction and Forgetting

Hi everyone,

Welcome to **Unit 5 of Introduction to Cognitive Psychology**!

First, **congratulations on completing your Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ) assessment**, and thank you for contributing to the discussions and engaging with one another’s explanations of change blindness. I will be reviewing your submissions and preparing your grades and feedback this week.

Building on last week’s exploration of long-term memory and knowledge structures, we now turn to **how we learn, why we forget, and how our memories can be reconstructed**. Two useful questions to keep in mind are: **How can we make learning more effective, and how can we judge the reliability of what we remember?**

This week, we will explore three connected themes:

- **Memory encoding and effective learning:** We will examine how new memories are formed, what research on anterograde amnesia can tell us about difficulties forming new long-term memories, and how levels of processing, retrieval practice, spaced study and dual coding relate to learning.
- **Reconstructive memory and false memories:** We will consider how existing knowledge and information encountered after an event can shape our recollections, including the implications for eyewitness testimony.
- **Emotion and memory:** We will explore how emotion influences learning and remembering, alongside the roles of different brain regions and their interactions.
