# Unit 2 Welcome Email

**Subject:** Welcome to Unit 2 – Visual Perception and Attention

Hi everyone,

Welcome to Unit 2 of Introduction to Cognitive Psychology!

This week, we turn to **visual perception and attention**, exploring how the brain processes visual information and, perhaps more surprisingly, how much of the world around us we can fail to notice.

We often assume that seeing something means we are consciously aware of it. However, psychological research suggests that our visual experience is far less complete than it feels. Our attention is selective, and even quite substantial changes in our surroundings can go unnoticed.

In this unit, we'll explore three interconnected topics:

- **Two Visual Systems theory:** How different visual processing pathways support the perception of objects and the visual guidance of actions.
- **Selective attention and change blindness:** How attention determines what we consciously notice, and why we sometimes miss surprisingly obvious changes.
- **Attention and consciousness:** The relationship between processing visual information and becoming consciously aware of it.

These topics introduce some fundamental questions about how our minds construct our experience of the world.

## This week's practical activity: The Change Blindness Experiment

One of the most interesting activities this week is the **Change Blindness Experiment**, which you'll find in the [Psychology Technical Hub](https://www.my-course.co.uk/mod/folder/view.php?id=946720).

In this experiment, you'll see two nearly identical visual scenes presented in quick succession, separated by a brief interruption. Your task is simply to identify what has changed.

It sounds straightforward, but you may find it surprisingly difficult!

**Please complete this experiment during Unit 2. Participation is required in preparation for your Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ) assessment, which begins in Unit 3.**

## Reflective exercise

Once you've completed the experiment, spend a little time thinking about the following questions:

1. **Spotting the difference:** How long did it take you to identify the change? Did you use any particular strategies to search the scene?
2. **Attentional limitations:** What does your performance suggest about the limitations of human attention? Can you think of everyday situations where these limitations might matter?
3. **Explaining change blindness:** How might you explain your experience using the theories and research introduced in this week's readings? In particular, what role does selective attention play?
4. **Preparing for the CLQ:** Make a few notes on your experience, your possible explanations and any relevant research. These will provide a useful starting point for your CLQ assessment next week.

## Connecting the experiment to the readings

As you work through the Unit 2 readings, I'd particularly encourage you to look at **Rensink, O'Regan and Clark (1997)**, whose classic research demonstrates the importance of attention in detecting changes within visual scenes.

The readings by **Posner (2012)** on attentional networks and consciousness, and **McConkie and Loschky** on change blindness, will help you develop a deeper understanding of the phenomenon. The work of **Milner and Goodale** also provides a broader perspective on the distinction between visual perception and visually guided action.

The relevant chapters of Eysenck and Keane (2020), Chapters 2, 4 and 5, provide further background.

As you read, try to move beyond simply describing what happened in the experiment and towards explaining **why it happened**. What does the evidence tell us about the relationship between attention, visual processing and conscious awareness?

This distinction between describing a phenomenon and explaining it through psychological theory will be particularly important for your CLQ.
