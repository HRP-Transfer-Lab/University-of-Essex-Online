# Unit 4 Welcome Email

**Subject:** Welcome to Unit 4 – Long-Term Memory and your CLQ peer responses

Hi everyone,

Welcome to **Unit 4 of Introduction to Cognitive Psychology**. This is also the **second and final week of your Collaborative Learning Question (CLQ)**, so please make time to read your classmates’ initial posts and complete your two peer responses.

This week, we move from attention and working memory to **how knowledge and experiences are stored, organised and used over the longer term**. We will explore:

- **Declarative (explicit) memory**, including episodic memory for events and semantic memory for facts and concepts.
- **Semantic networks and knowledge structures**, including propositions, schemas and scripts that help us organise information and make sense of familiar situations.
- **Non-declarative (implicit) memory**, including skills, implicit learning and priming.

The lecturecasts, *The Predictive Brain* and *Interactions in Long Term Memory*, also introduce predictive processing and the reconstructive nature of memory. A useful question to keep in mind is: **How might what we already know shape what we notice, expect and later remember?**

As you work through the readings and lecturecasts, consider how these ideas might help you evaluate explanations of change blindness. Be selective: use a concept where the evidence supports its relevance to the particular explanation you are discussing.

**Your CLQ priority: two peer responses**

Your 500-word initial response was due during Unit 3. This week, you need to respond to **two different classmates**, posting **300 words per response by the end of Unit 4**. Label these **Peer Response 1** and **Peer Response 2**.

In your responses:

- Engage directly with a specific explanation or claim in your classmate’s post.
- Critically evaluate or extend that explanation using research from **two of the following approaches: cognitive psychology, cognitive neuroscience and cognitive neuropsychology**.
- Explain what the evidence adds, how it supports or challenges the argument, and any relevant limitations.
- Draw on relevant resources from **Units 1–4**, maintain an academic tone and use **APA referencing**, or your approved programme referencing style where applicable.

You can agree with a classmate and still offer a critical response. For example, you might explain why their account is convincing, then use another source of evidence to identify what remains unresolved. Aim to develop the discussion through a reasoned contribution.

**All three assessed posts are required to pass the CLQ.** Enter your writing directly into the discussion forum, keep to the specified word counts, and check the deadline shown on the module site. Posts submitted after the deadline will not be assessed. You can also contribute up to five informal posts to support discussion; these are outside the assessed word count.
